Java was Hindu-Buddhist for 700 years — Borobudur Buddhist, Prambanan Shaiva, 240 temples in one plain. By 1293, Majapahit ruled 98 tributaries from Sumatra to Maluku. Nagarakretagama lists them.

But Majapahit's ports — Demak, Banten — traded pepper to Muslims. Imams preached, kings converted for the monsoon network. After 1402, Malacca — the new strait power — was Muslim.

Demak took Majapahit around 1527. Java's courts became mosques. Bali didn't.

Bali was Majapahit's *far east* — the *subak* irrigation guilds, each temple-managed, already ran the rice terraces. Priests carried *lontar* palm manuscripts — Adiparva, Ramayana in Kawi — across the Bali Strait with reformer Dang Hyang Nirartha.

They didn't recreate Majapahit. They ran *Agama Hindu Dharma*: same Shiva/Buddha syncretism (Bhinneka Tunggal Ika — 'diverse, one'), but now village-based, not state. Every household shrine faces Gunung Agung, every rice terrace prays to Dewi Danu.

So Java prayed to Allah for the pepper trade. Bali kept Shiva for the water.

That's why Borobudur is a museum and Besakih is a kitchen.
