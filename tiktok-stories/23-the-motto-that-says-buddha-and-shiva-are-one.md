## Script

Look at the national emblem of Indonesia. A golden bird grips a white scroll in its claws. On the scroll, three words: **Bhinneka Tunggal Ika**. Unity in diversity. Every government office in the country shows it.

Now the surprise. About **87 percent** of Indonesians are Muslim. And those words come from a Buddhist poem.

The poem is the ***Kakawin Sutasoma***, written in Old Javanese in the **14th century**, at the height of the **Majapahit** kingdom in Java. Most estimates put it between **1365 and 1389** ⚠verify. Its author, **Mpu Tantular**, is described as a Buddhist poet ⚠verify. The story is a Buddhist one. A prince gives his own body to a starving tigress. Then Tantular wrote one stanza that made the whole thing portable: Buddha and Shiva are two names for one truth.

Here is the stanza in one translation: "The well-known Buddha and Shiva are two different substances. They are indeed different, yet... the truth of Jina and the truth of Shiva is one." Different, and still one. ⚠verify

Why does this matter? Java had taken the whole Indian package: Sanskrit, Hindu gods, Buddhist scripture, a king who ruled as a god on earth. By the 1300s its poets were arguing that two of those religions were a single truth. That is Indianization doing something nobody planned. Java didn't just copy India. It made an argument about India, in its own language.

Then the story jumps six centuries. In **1945**, Indonesia is about to become independent. The nationalist **Mohammad Yamin** brings the phrase to a preparatory committee, after reading a Dutch scholar's writings on the poem. Who first rediscovered the text is contested. ⚠verify

On **11 February 1950**, the state emblem is adopted. Its bird is the **Garuda**: in Hindu myth, the mount of the god **Vishnu**. Garuda was carved on Java's temples long before anyone called it a national symbol. The first design had a human body and arms. Indonesia's Islamic party **Masyumi** objected that it was too mythical. So the bird was redrawn as a naturalistic eagle, based on a real hawk-eagle from Java. The motto stayed. Only the bird was reshaped. ⚠verify

And the bird never left. Indonesia's flagship airline is **Garuda Indonesia**. Thailand's royal emblem is a Garuda too. The same Indian bird now sits on the seals of two modern states that share no common religion.

So here is the reframe. Indonesia did not outgrow its Indian past to become a modern, mostly Muslim nation. It put a Buddhist argument for religious unity, from a Hindu-Buddhist court, on the seal of the state. Indianization didn't end when the kingdoms fell. It moved onto the government's seal.

## Accuracy flags

- **Who rediscovered the text.** The motto article credits the Dutch orientalist Kern (*Verspreide Geschriften*) as the route by which Yamin found it. The emblem article credits Brandes, who recovered manuscripts from the Lombok treasure, looted in 1894. The two Wikipedia pages do not reconcile these. Check with a scholarly source before saying who "found" it. ⚠verify
- **Date of the poem.** The Sutasoma article gives "most probably between 1365 and 1389" with no primary citation. Other pages say only "14th century." ⚠verify
- **Translation.** Motto article: "Unity in Diversity," also "Although diverse, both truthful to Dharma." Sutasoma article: "(Although) in pieces, yet One." Mpu Tantular article: "The Diversity of One." Four translations, no agreed one. The script quotes one translation and marks the disagreement. ⚠verify
- **Mpu Tantular as Buddhist.** The Wikipedia page says he was a Buddhist and "respectful to other religions." The source is a Wikipedia summary; check against Zoetmulder or Santoso before the claim is final. ⚠verify
- **Masyumi objection.** The emblem article says Masyumi objected that the human-shaped Garuda was "too mythical," but the claim is unreferenced in the fetched text. ⚠verify
- **Thai Garuda.** The emblem article mentions in passing that Thailand's emblem "also adopted the Garuda as a supporter." I did not fetch the Thai emblem page. ⚠verify
- **87% Muslim.** The 2025 figure (87.15%) is from Ministry of Home Affairs data as reported on Wikipedia. It is not verified against the ministry directly. The Garuda and motto facts are from the emblem and motto pages, not from the vault. Not flagged separately; it is the basis of the hook.
- **Not claimed:** that the motto was drafted by a Hindu poet, that Indonesia is the world's largest Muslim-majority state, or that Garuda Indonesia's founding year is a fact. These are not in the fetched pages and are left out.
