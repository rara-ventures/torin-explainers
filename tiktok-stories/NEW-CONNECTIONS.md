# New Connections — What the Two-Hop Crawl Joined

Thirteen links that did not exist in the vault before this work. 1–10 came from the two-hop crawl; 11–13 are reversals found on the third review pass — vectors that run **out of** Southeast Asia, or that the vault never had at all.

---

## 1. Camphor is the reason a Tamil stone sits in Sumatra — and the reason the 1025 raid worked

**Joins:** [[Trade Maritime — Vector]] · [[Kedah Srivijaya — Region]] · [[Straits Archipelago — Region]]

Camphor and benzoin came out of the northern Sumatran hinterland through **Barus** on the west coast and **Pannai** on the east. The Lobu Tua charter of **1088** records the Ayyavole 500 guild meeting at Barus. Rajendra's fleet resupplied at Barus, ran south, and came at Palembang through the **Sunda Strait** — from behind, while Srivijaya's navy sat at Kedah guarding Malacca.

**The link nobody had made:** the commodity route *was* the invasion route. Merchant logistics, not naval science.
**Filmable line:** *"The empire that couldn't hold a port still found the back door — because traders had already mapped it."*
**Pannai's only surviving trace:** 16 Vajrayana temples at Padang Lawas, chiefly Bahal.

## 2. The raid → the refugee → the monastery → the next Javanese century

**Joins:** [[Kedah Srivijaya — Timeline]] · [[Sailendra Mataram — Region]] · [[Majapahit — Region]]

1025: the mahārājā is captured at Kadaram. His daughter escapes the sack of Palembang, reaches the East Javanese court of **Airlangga** (born in Bali, 1002), becomes his queen as Dharmaprasadottuṅgadevī, and in **1035 Airlangga founds a monastery named Śrīvijayāśrama — "Srivijaya's hermitage" — after her homeland.** The raid breaks the toll monopoly; Kahuripan and then Kediri fill the gap with agrarian power; Majapahit inherits it.

**The link:** a Buddhist monastery in Java, named after a destroyed Sumatran empire, built by a king who married the refugee — that is the whole archipelagic reshuffle in one object.
**Filmable line:** *"India's only overseas invasion didn't create an Indian colony. It created a Javanese one."*

## 3. The god-king's itinerary is Java first, not Angkor first

**Joins:** [[Brahmin Royal — Vector]] · [[Sailendra Mataram — Region]] · [[Angkor — Region]] · [[Sukhothai Ayutthaya — Region]]

The crawl's *devarāja* and *cakravartin* material puts adoption in order: **Javanese courts first, then Malay, then Khmer, then Thai monarchy.** The vault's own Angkor reading (Kulke 1978; Jacques 1985) holds that Sanskrit *devarāja* glosses the Khmer institution *kamrateṅ jagat ta rāja* — so the direction of the idea is contested, but the direction of the **grades** is not: *cakravartin* came in three sizes (universal monarch / one-continent ruler / local king), which is precisely why any surplus-holding polity could buy the one that fitted.

**Filmable line:** *"A king could shop for legitimacy. There were three sizes."*
**Caution:** do not assert the diffusion order as settled.

## 4. There is no Indian administration in Southeast Asia — but there is Indian corporate law on stone

**Joins:** [[Law Dharma — Vector]] · [[Straits Archipelago — Region]] · [[Kedah Srivijaya — Region]]

The vault's claim "Tamil merchant guilds" had no named inscription behind it. It does now: **Takuapa** (c. 9th c.: a lake, Avani-nāraṇam, placed under the Maniggirāmam guild's protection), **Lobu Tua** (1088, Ayyavole 500), **Neusu** (12th c.: interest waived in kind, the royal emblem *ilacchinal* ← Sanskrit *lañcana*, enforcement on "our people", *makkal*), and the **Viraraghava copper plate** (c. 1225: 72 royal privileges granted to the Manigramam, whose leadership passed by corporate succession inside a merchant oligarchy, not father to son).

**The link:** the missing "Indian state" in Southeast Asia is replaced by Indian *companies* with charters, flags (the Ayyavole bull), their own merchant law (*Vira-baṇajā-dharma*), and four names in four languages.
**Filmable line:** *"Medieval Asia had multinational corporations. They left their contracts in stone, and they're still on display."*

## 5. Meru travelled farther than any Indian army

**Joins:** [[Art Architecture — Vector]] · [[China — Region]] · [[Himalaya — Region]] · [[Pagan — Region]]

Angkor Wat's five towers are Meru's peaks and its moat the cosmic ocean; Borobudur's ten terraces climb the three *dhātus*; the **Burmese pyatthat** roof's finial represents Meru; the **Chinese pagoda sits on a Sumeru throne** (xūmízuò); Taoist scripture absorbs the mountain after Buddhism reaches China. One imaginary peak, five architectural traditions, no garrison anywhere.

**The link:** cosmology is the only Indian export that demonstrably moved *north into China*, which no army or monastery network did.
**Filmable line:** *"A mountain that doesn't exist ended up on a Chinese pagoda, a Burmese temple and the world's largest religious monument."*

## 6. The court went Indian. The ritual stayed female.

**Joins:** [[Philippines — Region]] · [[Champa — Region]] · [[Bali — Region]] · [[Angkor — Region]]

Everywhere in the region, the imported layer is a **male court specialty** — Brahmin consecrators, Sanskrit titles, royal monasteries. The ritual substrate did not move. In the Philippines the specialists were *babaylan*, *balian* and *katalonan*, **overwhelmingly women**, with effeminate men (*asog*, *bayok*) also serving; Indian vocabulary attached to *diwata* and *anito* without importing a priesthood. In Champa the local goddess **Po Nagar / Bhagavatī eclipses the Śaiva liṅga after 784**. At Angkor, *neak ta* ancestor shrines sit beside Buddhist *wats* and Brahmin *bakou* families still officiate at court rites. In Bali, *pedanda Siwa* and *pedanda Boda* run as parallel priestly lines.

**The link:** this is the strongest single argument against "India converted Southeast Asia" — the religions people actually practised kept their own gender order.
**Filmable line:** *"Kings hired Indian priests. Villages kept their priestesses. Only one of those survived the empires."*

## 7. The Rāmāyaṇa circled the region; it did not radiate from India

**Joins:** [[Sailendra Mataram — Region]] · [[Angkor — Region]] · [[Sukhothai Ayutthaya — Region]] · [[Kedah Srivijaya — Region]] · [[Philippines — Region]]

Java writes the *Kakawin Rāmāyaṇa* c. **856–930**. Cambodia's earliest allusion is a 7th-century stone (K.359) but the surviving *Reamker* poem is 16th-century. Thailand's court *Ramakien* is 18th-century. The Maranao *Maharadia Lawana* may have arrived in the **17th–19th centuries**. And the theatre forms move *backwards* too: southern Thai **nang talung** is the parent of Malaysian **wayang gedek**, traditionally performed in Kedah — an Indian epic on a stage, crossing a border that only exists since 1909.

**The link:** the direction of travel is intra-regional, and the latest versions are the ones with the biggest national reputations.
**Filmable line:** *"Six countries claim this epic as theirs. Java had it 850 years before Thailand."* Full script: [[11-three-hundred-ramayanas]].

## 8. The 1025 coincidence: the raid and the relay leave Sumatra in the same year

**Joins:** [[Kedah Srivijaya — Timeline]] · [[Buddhism — Vector]] · [[Himalaya — Region]]

**1025** is the year Rajendra's fleet sacks Palembang, *and* the year the Bengali scholar **Atisha** begins his return journey from Sumatra, where he had studied under Dharmakirti of Suvarṇadvipa. He goes to Vikramaśīla, then to Tibet in 1042, and founds the Kadampa lineage that reshapes Tibetan Buddhism.

**The link:** the node that transmitted Vajrayana to Tibet was being bombed as its most consequential student left it.
**Filmable line:** *"The year the fleet arrived was the year the monk left. Tibet's Buddhism was already on the boat."*
**Confidence:** medium — the year is a reconstruction, not an inscription.

## 9. Names are the fossil record of this whole exchange

**Joins:** [[Funan Chenla — Region]] · [[Champa — Region]] · [[Philippines — Region]] · [[Kedah Srivijaya — Region]] · [[Rome Byzantium — Region]]

Almost everything in the early record is a name, not an event: **Kataha / Kalah / Kadaram** (Kedah in Sanskrit, Arabic and Tamil); **Sanfoqi**, which after 1025 probably means Jambi wearing Srivijaya's label; **Rajah Kiling**, where *Kiling* ≈ *Keling*, a word for an Indian; **Butban** on the Butuan ivory seal; **Avani-nāraṇam**, a lake named after a Pallava king's title, which is how we date a Thai inscription; **Ma-i**, whose location depends on whether a scribe read a Hokkien character in the literary or the vernacular voice.

**The link:** toponyms and titles are doing the work of historiography in a region with almost no local chronicles — which is also why they can be wrong.
**Filmable line:** *"We know these kingdoms existed because of their names. That's also why we keep getting them wrong."*

## 10. Repatriation is the same story in two directions

**Joins:** [[Kedah Srivijaya — Region]] · [[Philippines — Region]] · [[resources/Watch & Listen]]

The **Leiden copper plates** of Rajaraja I — the charter for a Sumatran king's Buddhist monastery in Tamil Nadu — were in a Dutch university collection **until they were restituted to India in 2026**. The **Agusan image**, found in Mindanao in 1917, was sold to the Field Museum in 1922 for ₱4,000 and is still in Chicago, with a standing repatriation campaign.

**The link:** one object went home this year; the other has been waiting for a century, and the reason is the difference between a European collection in India and an American collection in a colony.
**Filmable line:** *"Two objects, two museums, two centuries. One just went home."*

## Pass 3 — the three reversals

The first two passes found connections *within* the Indianization frame. This pass broke the frame. Each of these is a vector the vault did not have.

### 11. Betel runs the other way — and it is 2,000 years older than Indianization

**Joins (new): Island SE Asia → Sri Lanka → India → Persia → the Mediterranean.** Not in the vault at all.

The areca palm is native to Island Southeast Asia; the betel leaf (*Piper betle*, pepper family) is too. The dated diffusion, from archaeology and chemistry rather than inscriptions: nut traces in **pre-agricultural forager** dental calculus on Sulawesi (**25,000–16,000 BP**); the earliest full quid with lime at **Duyong Cave, Palawan, 4,630 ± 250 BP**; Micronesia and Near Oceania 3,500–3,000 BP; Lapita 3,600–2,500 BP; **mainland SE Asia 3,000–2,500 BP; South India and Sri Lanka by ~3,500 BP; northern India by 1,500 BP; Madagascar by 600 BP** — and from India westward into Persia and the Mediterranean. The word follows the plant: English *areca* is from **Dravidian** (Malayalam *aṭaykka*, Kannada *aḍike*, Tamil *aḍaikkāy*), not Sanskrit. Proto-Austronesian **\*buaq** ("fruit") narrowed to mean the nut in Proto-Malayo-Polynesian.

**Why this is the biggest find of the crawl:** the habit reached South Asia roughly two thousand years before the script did, carried by the same Austronesian sailors whose trade routes later carried Brahmins. ~600 million people chew it today. Script: [[12-betel-runs-the-other-way]].
**Source:** Zumbroich, *eJournal of Indian Medicine* 1 (2007–08).

### 12. Games: the seventh vector nobody has written

**Joins (new): India → Mainland SE Asia, still played.** Zero hits in the vault for *chess*, *chaturanga*, *makruk*, *ouk*.

Thai **makruk** descends from sixth–seventh-century Indian *chaturanga*, and its piece names are a localisation you can read off the board: *khun* (lord), **met (seed)** — the queen-equivalent, still moving one diagonal square like the Persian *ferz*, an upgrade Europe applied c. 1475 and Thailand never took; *khon* (nobleman), *ma* (horse), **ruea ("boat")** where Europe put a castle, and **bia ("cowrie shell")** for the pawns — money as infantry, in sets actually cut from cowrie and buffalo horn. Cambodian **ouk chaktrang** keeps the word *chaturanga* alive in Khmer, with 12th-century bas-relief attestation. Murray argued it travelled **with Buddhism** — the same vector as the script. Plus a rule no Western version has: endgame **counting**, where the winning side must mate inside a fixed move budget or the game is drawn.

**Why it matters for the vault:** the six vectors (script, kingship, law, religion, art, trade) miss leisure entirely, and leisure is the most filmable transfer there is. Script: [[13-the-chessboard-is-a-fossil]].

### 13. Indianized polities invaded India. Twice, in the same century.

**Joins:** [[Sri Lanka — Region]] · 13th-century timeline · [[Mainland SE Asia — Region]] · [[Kedah Srivijaya — Region]] — and this was **missing from the vault**.

**1247:** **Chandrabhanu Sridhamaraja** of **Tambralinga** (Nakhon Si Thammarat) sends a fleet to Sri Lanka; the chronicle gives the aim as acquiring the **Buddhist relic**. Repelled by Prince Veerabahu under Parakramabahu II.
**1255:** he returns and **occupies the Jaffna kingdom** in the Tamil north, holding it seven years.
**1258:** **Jatavarman Sundara Pandyan I** lands, reduces Jaffna to Pandyan suzerainty.
**1262:** Chandrabhanu killed. His son **Savakanmaindan** continues with Chola, Pandyan and Sinhalese allies until defeat in the late 1270s. Thirty years of attempts.

**Why it matters:** the Tooth Relic is already in the vault as the palladium of Sri Lankan kingship. The missing half is that a peninsular king used the *same* relic theory of legitimacy to attack the island — Indianized states were not receivers of a package, they were competitors inside it. Script: [[14-the-king-who-sailed-west]].

### Accuracy flag from pass 3

The entry article's Malaysia section dates **Chandrabhanu's attacks on Sri Lanka to the 11th century** and calls him a Ligor king who "attacked Sri Lanka in the 11th century, an event noted in a stone inscription in Nagapattinum and in the Mahavamsa." The chronicle and epigraphic sequence is **1247 → late 1270s**, i.e. thirteenth century. The entry page is off by two hundred years — which is exactly the kind of error the two-hop method catches and the one-hop read would have repeated.
