## Script

Ask most people how India reached Southeast Asia and you get kings, priests, armies. The Indian state never conquered Thailand. So what actually crossed the sea?

Here is one answer. It's a stone from around 850, found on the Takuapa coast in southern Thailand, on the Andaman side of the isthmus. It's written in Tamil, and it records a lake.

The lake is called **Avani-nāraṇam**. A man named Nāṅgūr-Udaiyan dug it. And then the stone does something no royal inscription is supposed to do. It places the lake under the protection of the **Māṇiggirāmam**, a merchant guild living in the military camp.

Stop there. One man digs a water supply. A company is handed the guard duty. That is not a king's decree. That is a contract with a corporate guardian, cut in stone.

Why would traders take on a lake? Because they lived there, they needed water, and they had the most to lose if it failed. The guild was not visiting. It was a permanent Tamil presence on the isthmus.

Now the date. The name gives the stone away. *Avani-nāraṇa* is a royal title, and the scholars who read it tie it to the Pallava king Nandivarman III of Kanchipuram, who reigned in the mid-800s ⚠verify (c. 846–869). So a Tamil lake in Thailand is dated by a surname from southern India.

It's not a one-off. By 1088, the Ayyavole 500, another great Tamil merchant guild, is meeting at Barus on Sumatra's west coast, the camphor port. Two and a half centuries later, the same kind of business is still being written down in the same language.

And these guilds were not loose networks. The Ayyavole 500 guarded a code called the *Vira-baṇajā-dharma*, the law of noble merchants. They flew a bull on their flag. A copper plate from around 1225 gives the Manigramam 72 royal privileges, ⚠verify the count.

Charter. Flag. Internal law. Privileges. Guard duties. Enforcement carved in stone.

Here's the reveal. The Indian state stayed home. What went across the sea was the company form, the one that still runs trade today.

So the next time someone says India exported gods and kings to Southeast Asia, ask them who was guarding the lake.
