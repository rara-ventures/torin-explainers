## Script

Thailand's king is called Rama. That is his regnal title, the name he reigns under. The current monarch is Rama X, the tenth king of the Chakri dynasty, which began in 1782.

Where does the name come from? Go back to 1351. A king named Ramathibodi founds a new capital where the Lopburi river meets the Chao Phraya. He calls it Ayutthaya. Say it out loud. That is Ayodhya, the northern Indian city where Rama is a prince, exiled and then returning to take the throne.

Why build a new city and name it after a faraway one? Because naming is a claim. A new capital is a new beginning, and the Ramayana gives it a template: a rightful king, a lost kingdom, a return. Ramathibodi was drawing a river into a new kind of state, pulling in Lavo and Suphan Buri, and the name told everyone that this was the Ayodhya of their age.

The claim was not only in the name. Ramathibodi is credited with the Thammasat, a law code built on the Indian Dharmasastra and recast in Pali Buddhist terms. Manu stays as an ancestral authority. Caste is not adopted. The Brahmins are there to perform the coronation, but the law runs on merit and royal edict. The Indian package is kept, and reshaped to fit.

Ayutthaya held the river for four centuries. Then in 1767 the Konbaung army took the city after a fourteen-month siege and burned it. The court moved to Thonburi, then to Bangkok. Ayutthaya's kings had carried Rama in their names, starting with Ramathibodi himself. Then, in the 1910s, King Vajiravudh gave every Chakri king a number after Rama, going back to the founder ⚠verify. The city was gone. The name moved with the throne.

So the Thai king is named after an Indian hero. His old capital was named after the Indian hero's city. The national epic came later, performed as masked *khon*. The name was never ornament. It was a way of saying: this is the new Ayodhya, and I am its king.

## Production notes

- **Angle:** Deliberately not the Ramayana-versions angle used in [[11-three-hundred-ramayanas]]. This one is about the title and the capital: a king re-founds a state by re-naming it after an Indian city.
- **Correction to the VIRAL-CONNECTIONS brief:** the brief says "the Thai state is named after an Indian city." Only the capital was. The country was called Siam for most of its history. The script uses the accurate version.
- **Date check:** the region file says Ayutthaya lasted "417 years"; 1351 to 1767 is 416 years. The script says "four centuries," so no conflict.
- **Ramathibodi's name:** the script glosses it as "Rama, lord." Keep the pronunciation simple on screen.
- **Rama numbering:** the "Rama I…X" style is a 20th-century convention (Vajiravudh/Rama VI), applied backwards. Confirm before filming; don't claim the 1782 founder called himself Rama I.
- **Caution:** the Sukhothai "golden age" and the Ramkhamhaeng stele are contested, so this script does not rely on them.
