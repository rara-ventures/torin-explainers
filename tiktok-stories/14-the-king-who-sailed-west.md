## Script

The whole story of Indianization is one-directional. India sends gods, scripts, kingship, law. Southeast Asia receives them. That framing has an exception, and it's an invasion.

First you need to know what a tooth is doing in this story. In Sri Lanka the **Buddha's Tooth Relic** isn't a museum piece. It's the deed to the throne. The chronicle tradition makes the relic the palladium of kingship: when Parakramabahu I retrieved it in **1157**, he did it to secure his own accession. Whoever holds the tooth, holds the island. That idea is an Indian-origin package — relic cult plus *cakravartin* kingship — and Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia both run on it.

Which means it was lootable. And someone from Southeast Asia went to loot it.

**Tambralinga.** A Malay peninsula kingdom centred on what is now Nakhon Si Thammarat in southern Thailand, controlling both coasts of the isthmus, and the same port that had been under Srivijaya and was claimed by the Cholas in 1025. By the thirteenth century it's Buddhist, it calls itself a *dhammaraja* polity, and its ruler Chandrabhanu — *Chandrabhanu Sridhamaraja* — is described in the sources as a **Javaka**, tied to the Javanese world.

**1247.** Chandrabhanu sends a fleet to Sri Lanka. The stated purpose is to acquire the Buddhist relic. The Sinhalese king Parakramabahu II sends his nephew, Prince Veerabahu, who pushes the invaders back. It looks like a failed raid.

**1255.** He tries again, and this time he wins in the north. Using **poison darts**, Tambralinga forces occupy the **Jaffna kingdom**, the Tamil north of the island. A peninsular Southeast Asian king is now ruling Sri Lankan territory, and he keeps it for seven years, while repeated attempts continue against the Sinhalese south.

**1258.** The south Indian answer arrives. The Pandyan emperor **Jatavarman Sundara Pandyan I** invades the island. Chandrabhanu submits — and Jaffna passes from Tambralinga into Pandyan suzerainty. Note what just happened: a Southeast Asian conquest of Sri Lankan territory got absorbed by a South Indian dynasty, and the region's politics fused.

**1262.** Chandrabhanu is killed, by the brother of the Pandyan commander. He had spent **more than thirty years** trying to take Sri Lanka.

**1262–1270.** The story doesn't end with him. His son **Savakanmaindan** keeps at it, leading Tambralinga forces together with Chola and Pandyan soldiers and two Sinhalese princes — a coalition that only makes sense if Tamil and peninsular politics had already intermarried — until the Pandyans under Jatavarman Vira Pandyan I break that effort, and then again in the late 1270s under Maravarman Kulasekara Pandyan I.

Now, why isn't this in the standard account? Because it breaks the shape of the story. The Indianized states of Southeast Asia are usually cast as receivers — passive recipients of a superior culture. But Chandrabhanu was *using the Indian playbook against India's own heir*. He had the same relic theory, the same *cakravartin* claims, the same Buddhist legitimacy logic, and he deployed a fleet to take it. That's not receiving civilisation. That's competing inside it.

And the relic logic went right back into the mainland. Nakhon Si Thammarat is the same port from which, decades later, a Sri Lankan monk, **Sumana Thera**, carries the Lankavamsa ordination line back into Thai Buddhism — the vault already has that row for Sukhothai. So the peninsula sits between two histories: exporting a king who invaded Sri Lanka, and importing the reformed sangha from it.
