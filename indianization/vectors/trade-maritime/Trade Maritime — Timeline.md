# Timeline — Vector: Monsoon Trade & Port-Polities

| Year | Event | Indianization Link | Source | Confidence |
|------|-------|--------------------|--------|------------|
| c. 40–70 CE | **Periplus Maris Erythraei** names Muziris and Barygaza and credits Hippalus with the open-ocean crossing | Roman–Indian monsoon route; the handbook's knowledge fades east of the Bay of Bengal | Periplus §54, 57; Casson 1989 | high |
| c. 150 CE | **Ptolemy** places Kattigara at the eastern limit of the inhabited world | Greek geography reaches the South China Sea; identification with Óc Eo probable, not proven | Ptolemy, *Geog.* 1.14 | medium |
| 2nd–3rd c. | **Óc Eo** imports Indian beads, seals and gold foil, plus Roman medallions of Antoninus Pius | Goods and icons arrive together at Funan's entrepôt; no Indian administration | Malleret 1942; Manguin 2004 | high |
| 166 | Roman embassy (Andun) reaches the Han court via Jiaozhou, probably through the Mekong delta | Direct Rome–China contact by sea, bypassing India | *Hou Hanshu*; Coedès 1968 | medium |
| 225–243 | Funan sends embassies to Wu; Kang Tai and Zhu Ying report its ports and wealth | Delta polity plugged into Chinese trade, Indian forms already present | *Liang Shu*; Coedès 1968 | high |
| 5th c. | **Kantoli** in southern Sumatra sends missions to Liu Song (454–464, 519) | Sumatran port-polity enters the China trade before Srivijaya | Coedès 1968 | medium |
| 5th–6th c. | **Bujang Valley/Sungai Batu**: iron furnaces, a jetty and a brick monument; Indian ritual from the fifth century | Kedah as the peninsula's Bay of Bengal port; Tamil goods and cult | Jacq-Hergoualc'h 2002; Murphy 2018 | medium |
| 671 | **Yijing** spends six months at Srivijaya and finds a thousand monks studying Sanskrit | Monastic network carries texts and prestige, not only cargo | Yijing; Wolters 1967 | high |
| 682/683 | **Kedukan Bukit** inscription: Dapunta Hyang's *siddhayatra*, Srivijaya founded near Palembang | Old Malay in Pallava-derived script; Sanskrit title, local oath | Kedukan Bukit; Coedès 1968 | high |
| 775 | **Ligor B** inscription in Kawi records Mahārāja Dharmasetu's sanctuaries on the peninsula | Srivijayan mandala extends over the Kra approaches | Ligor inscription; Coedès 1968 | high |
| 860 | **Balaputra** endows a monastery at Nalanda; Sumatra–Bengal monastic link | Sailendra/Srivijayan king patronizes Indian Buddhism directly | Nalanda inscription; Coedès 1968 | high |
| 1025 | **Rājendra I**'s Chola fleet raids Palembang and Kadaram | Plunder breaks Srivijayan toll power; initiative shifts to the Coromandel | Tanjore inscription; Coedès 1968 | high |
| 1349 | **Wang Dayuan**'s *Daoyi Zhilüe* describes Temasek/Long Ya Men and Straits trade | Chinese ceramics and Straits entrepôts after Srivijaya | Wang Dayuan 1349; Heng 2009 | medium |
| c. 1400 | **Melaka** founded by Parameswara; four shahbandars and the *Undang-Undang Laut Melaka* | Sanskrit titles and port law survive the conversion to Islam c. 1414 | *Sejarah Melayu*; Heng 2009 | medium |
| 1511 | **Albuquerque** captures Melaka for Portugal | Portuguese inherit the choke point; the Indic court style ends | Coedès 1968; Heng 2009 | high |
