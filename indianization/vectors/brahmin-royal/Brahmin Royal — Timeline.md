# Timeline — Vector: Brahmins, Devaraja & Cakravartin

| Year | Event | Indianization Link | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2nd–3rd c. (contested: 4th–5th) | Võ Cạnh C.40 stele at Nha Trang names King Śrī Māra in a 2.5 m Sanskrit eulogy; no priest is named. Coedès tied it to Funan's first wave; Zakharov and Sircar read Gupta-era metre and vocabulary and date it later. | Sanskrit panegyric arrives before any documented Brahmin household — the idiom precedes the specialist. | Coedès 1968; Zakharov 2010 | medium |
| 5th c. | The Chinese *Book of Liang* reports a Brahmin from India, Chiao Chen-ju (Kaundinya), invited to reign at Funan; he "changed all the laws to conform to the system of India." | Earliest textual claim that a Brahmin reordered a Southeast Asian kingdom's law — the kernel of the Kaundinya–Soma foundation myth. | *Book of Liang*, via Pelliot 1903 | medium |
| 380–413 | Bhadravarman I moves his capital to Siṃhapura, builds a liṅga hall at Mỹ Sơn for Śiva Bhadreśvara — the god made from his own name — and invites learned Brahmins from India. First Cham king to take the *-varman* suffix. | King and god fused in a single name; the *-varman* title and Sanskrit charter are borrowed from the Pallava court. | Coedès 1968; Finot 1904 | high |
| c. 450–490 | Vat Phou K.365: Devanika, "king of kings," comes from afar, founds Śreṣṭhapura and a sacred *tīrtha* on the Mekong beside liṅga-shaped Lingaparvata, and offers thousands of cattle. | A Brahminical *tīrtha* planted on a local spirit-mountain — the Kurukshetra model localized to legitimate a new dynasty. | Coedès 1956 (K.365) | high |
| 657 | Mỹ Sơn C.96, the stele of Prakāśadharma (Vikrantavarman I), traces the king's ancestry to the Brahmin Kauṇḍinya and the nāga princess Somā. | Genealogy as title deed: a Sanskrit pedigree converts a Cham house into a branch of the Funan foundation line. | Coedès 1968; Finot 1904 | high |
| 732 | Canggal inscription: Sañjaya installs a Śaiva liṅga on Gunung Wukir and declares himself universal ruler of Mataram. | The liṅga-and-praśasti package in Central Java; no Indian priest is named, but the liturgical form is imported whole. | de Casparis 1975; Coedès 1968 | high |
| 778 | Kalasan inscription: the *rājaguru* ("teacher of the king, jewel of the Śailendra family") persuades Mahārāja Panangkaran to build a Tārā temple and a Mahāyāna vihāra, endowing the village of Kalasa. | A hired royal preceptor directing royal building — the *purohita* as contractor, here Buddhist and writing in northern Prāṇāgarī. | Kalasan inscription 778; de Casparis 1975 | high |
| 802 | Jayavarman II's consecration on Mahendraparvata; K.235 says the Brahmin Hiraṇyadāman came from Janapada, taught four Tantric treatises, and produced the rite called *devarāja*. | The classic case: an imported Brahmin household installs a royal liṅga and a *cakravartin* claim that outlives its theology. | K.235 (1053); Coedès 1968 | medium |
| 914 | Belanjong pillar, Sanur: Śrī Keśarī Warmadewa records his campaigns in Sanskrit and Old Balinese, in Nāgarī and Kawi scripts. | Bali enters the record with the full *-varman*/*deva* title set — Brahminical titulature before a Brahmin caste. | Stutterheim 1934; Damais 1947 | high |
| 1053 (8 Feb) | K.235, Sdok Kok Thom: 340 lines in Sanskrit and Old Khmer recount nine generations of Śivakaivalya's family as royal chaplains and bar outsiders from serving the royal god. | Ritual expertise becomes hereditary property backed by land charters — the Brahmin household as a corporation. | K.235; Bhattacharya 2009 | high |
| 1056 | Shin Arahan, a Mon Theravāda monk, converts Anawrahta of Pagan; the Ari clergy are purged, but Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava and Brahmanical elements remain embedded in court and popular Buddhism. | Legitimation is a service, not a confession: the Theravāda turn keeps Brahmin astrology and consecration while demoting Śaivism. | Pe Maung Tin & Luce 1923; Aung-Thwin 1985 | low |
| 1113 / 1119 | Suryavarman II is enthroned; the aged Brahmin Divākarapaṇḍita officiates — his third royal coronation — and again at the formal rite in 1119. Angkor Wat is dedicated to Viṣṇu and the king becomes, posthumously, Paramaviṣṇuloka. | The *purohita* as kingmaker; the king absorbs the god, and the temple becomes his posthumous body. | Coedès 1968; Briggs 1951 | high |
| 1181 | Jayavarman VII is crowned after driving out the Cham; his Mahāyāna state keeps Brahmins at court, with Hṛṣīkeśa made chief priest under the title Jayamahāpradhāna. | A Buddhist regime retaining Brahmin consecration and hierarchy — cross-confessional continuity. | Coedès 1968; Sanderson 2003 | medium |
| c. 1351 | Ayutthaya is founded in the lower Chao Phraya; its Theravāda kings are consecrated by Brahmin *purohitas*, and Brahmin rites and astrologers remain part of court ceremony until 1767. | The repertoire outlives its host religion — Brahmin coronation becomes a Thai royal technology. | Baker & Phongpaichit 2017 | medium |
| c. 1492 (attested in Bali 1537) | Dang Hyang Nirartha, a Javanese Śaiva priest, arrives from Majapahit and is appointed *Bagawanta* (royal priest) at Gelgel; he founds the Śaiva *pedanda* lineages and promotes the *padmasana* shrine. | Java → Bali transfer of the priestly household; the priesthood hardens into a descent caste — the vector's fourth localization. | Pringle 2004; Vickers 1987 | medium |
