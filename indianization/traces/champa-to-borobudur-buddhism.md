# Trace: Champa (Vietnam) → Buddhism in Indonesia — From Po Nagar to Borobudur

> One vector (Mahayana/Vajrayana Buddhism), two coasts (Vietnam's Cham towers vs Java's mountain of stone), one bridge (Srivijaya's strait), 5th–9th centuries.

You asked to learn Champa → Buddhism in Indonesia. This trace is the syllabus.

## The Short Answer

Champa and Central Java did **not** share one Buddhist school. Champa stayed predominantly Shaiva (with *selective* Mahayana at Po Nagar/Indrapura), while Sailendra–Mataram Java swung hard to Mahayana/Vajrayana and built **Borobudur (2,672 panels, 504 Buddhas, ~35m high, 9 stacked mandalas)** as a three-dimensional sutra. The link is not doctrine — it's the *network*: Pallava script + Sanskrit + Nalanda monks + Srivijayan ports carried the same texts to both coasts, where local kings localized them differently.

Follow the 4 stops below in order.

---

## Stop 1 — Champa, Vietnam (c. 192–1471 CE)

**What it was:** Austronesian Cham-speaking federation (Indrapura near Da Nang, Vijaya near Quy Nhon) between Dai Viet and the sea. Not one kingdom but a mandala of *nagaras*.

**Indianization signature:** Sanskrit + Old Cham bilingual inscriptions (Pallava-Grantha → Cham script), *linga* of Bhadreshvara at **My Son A1**, and **Po Nagar (Kauthara)** — Shaiva tower that also housed Mahayana *Lokeshvara* images.

**Key sources for later filling:**

| Date | Source | What it says |
|------|--------|--------------|
| 2nd–3rd c. | **Vo Canh inscription** (Nha Trang) | Oldest Sanskrit in SE Asia — Sanskrit in Pallava-adjacent script; Hindu/Buddhist donor |
| 4th c. | **My Son E1 (Bhadravarman I)** | Earliest Champa Sanskrit stele, Shaiva |
| c. 618? | **My Son A1** | Bhadreshvara linga, Gupta–Pallava art |
| 7th–8th c. | **Po Nagar steles (C.96, C.30)** | Cham + Sanskrit, Shaiva *and* Buddhist patronage (Queen Po Nagar?) |
| 875 | **Indrapura (Dong Duong)** | Buddhist monastery — Mahayana/Vajrayana enclave in Shaiva Champa |
| 1177–1471 | **Songshi / Dai Viet chronicles** | Champa wars, tributary to China, fall of Vijaya 1471 to Le |

**Localized Buddhism:** Champa's Buddhism was *court* Buddhism (Indrapura's Dong Duong monastery under Indravarma II, Laksmindra Lokesvara) — never the population's religion. Compare to Java where Buddhism was *state* cosmology.

## Stop 2 — The Bridge: Srivijaya & Kedah (7th–13th c., Malay Peninsula + Sumatra)

**Why the bridge matters:** No monk sails directly from My Son to Borobudur. They sail My Son → Kedah → Palembang → Java, riding monsoon reversal.

- **Kedah (Kalah)** — entrepôt on peninsula, 6th c. proto-city, Yijing lands here 671.
- **Srivijaya (Palembang)** — 683 **Kedukan Bukit** inscription (Old Malay in Pallava script, *siddhayatra*, Buddhist *mahasiddha*), controls Malacca Strait, hosts Nalanda branch monastery ( Dharmakirti, later Atisha studies there 1011).

**The vector in action:** Srivijaya's monk **Yijing (635–713)** translates 400+ texts in Palembang after studying at Nalanda; his *Record of Buddhist Practices* describes Palembang as where "all students in Southeast Asia come to learn." A generation later, **Vajrabodhi (671–741)** stops in Srivijaya on his way to Tang China carrying Vajrayana.

> **Visualization:** `../visualizations/spread-map.md` — monsoon arrows: Amaravati → Kedah (1st–4th) → Srivijaya (7th) → both Champa and Java.

**Champa ↔ Srivijaya gap:** 8th c. Champa inscriptions note Javanese (*Java*) raids — Sailendra–Srivijaya naval rivalry over the same strait. Buddhism traveled *around* the warfare.

## Stop 3 — Sailendra–Mataram, Central Java (8th–10th c.)

**The mountain:** **Borobudur (~782–835, Sailendra)** — 9 stacked terraces (6 square + 3 circular), **2,672 relief panels** (Karmawibhangga, Lalitavistara, Gandavyuha), **504 Buddha statues**, 55,000 m³ stone. Not a *stupa* on a hill — a *mandala you walk* (pradaksina 5 km, three cosmological levels: Kamadhatu → Rupadhatu → Arupadhatu).

**Vectors converged here:**

- **Pala India (Nalanda) → Java:** **Kalasan inscription (778 Saka = 778 CE)** — Sailendra queen Tara builds Tara temple for Buddhist clergy, *guru* from Gaudi (Bengal) invited. **Kelurak (782)** links Buddhist and Shaiva lines. **Sojong** style = Pala bronze smallness blown to Javanese monumentality.
- **Pallava script:** Borobudur's Kawi script = Pallava-Grantha adapted; same family as My Son Cham script — *one script system, two languages, two religions*.

**Next door:** **Prambanan (c. 850, Sanjaya Hindu)** — 47m Shiva tower, Ramayana panels — built 30 km from Borobudur within decades. Same valley, two cosmologies. Sailendra used Buddhism to differentiate from Sanjaya's Shaiva legitimacy — *Indianization as political technology*.

## Stop 4 — The Divergence (Why Champa Stayed Shaiva, Java Went Buddhist, Then Both Went Elsewhere)

| Feature | Champa (Vietnam) | Central Java (Borobudur) |
|---------|------------------|--------------------------|
| **Dominant Indian idiom** | Shaiva *linga* cult (Bhadreshvara) + court Mahayana enclave | Mahayana/Vajrayana mandala cosmology |
| **Local substrate** | Austronesian *Po Yan Inu Nagar* → Hinduized as Po Nagar | Javanese *candi* mountain spirit → Buddhist stupa |
| **Script** | Cham (Old Cham + Sanskrit diglossia) | Kawi (Javanese + Sanskrit) — same Pallava parent |
| **Chinese witness** | Liangshu on Linyi (Champa) 3rd–6th c. | Yijing 671 on Srivijaya, Tang records on Heling (Java) |
| **Endgame** | Indrapura Buddhist peak 875 → re-Shaivaized 10th; Vijaya falls 1471 to Viet, Islam never roots | Borobudur abandoned 10th c. (volcano + eastward shift), Prambanan quake 16th; Islam via Demak from 15th; Bali carries Hindu residue |

**Theory to teach:** George Coedès' *Indianisation* vs Oliver Wolters' *localization* vs Sheldon Pollock's *Sanskrit cosmopolis*. Champa and Borobudur are **Pollock's cosmopolis at work** — Sanskrit as *prestige without empire* — but Wolters wins: locals chose *which* Indic idiom fit the hill they already worshipped.
