# Timeline — Sukhothai–Ayutthaya

| Year | Event | Indianization Link | Source | Confidence |
|------|-------|--------------------|--------|------------|
| c. 1200 CE | Khmer-Lavo town at Sukhothai: Ta Pha Daeng, Wat Phra Phai Luang, Wat Si Sawai already stand | Khmer (Lavo) Hindu-Buddhist landscape inherited by the Tai | Coedès 1968 | medium |
| 1238 CE | Si Inthrathit (Pho Khun Bang Klang Hao) expels the Lavo governor and founds the Phra Ruang dynasty | Tai kingship takes over an existing Indianized temple polity | Coedès 1968 | medium |
| c. 1280s CE | Ramkhamhaeng invites Lankavong monks from Nakhon Si Thammarat, builds monasteries for them, and sends monks on to Lanka | Sinhala Mahāvihāra lineage enters Sukhothai through the peninsula port | Jinakalamali (1516); Jermsawatdi 1979 | medium |
| 1283 CE | Sukhothai script (*Lai Sue Thai*) devised, per the Ramkhamhaeng stele | Brahmi via Old Khmer, recut for Tai phonology; above- and below-line vowels dropped | Griswold & Prasert 1971 | medium |
| 1292 CE* | Ramkhamhaeng stele installed — claims the Pali Tipitaka from Lanka via Nakhon Si Thammarat (Ligor); authenticity contested | Lanka → Sukhothai via Ligor (Lankavamsa) | Griswold & Prasert 1971; Vickery 1991 | low |
| 1319–1321 CE | Martaban breaks away (1319); Lan Na annexes Tak (1321) — Sukhothai contracts sharply | Loss of the Mon port cuts the Martaban leg of the Lankan relay | Coedès 1968 | high |
| 1345–1347 CE | Li Thai writes the *Traiphum Phra Ruang* as uparat, then accedes and shifts the centre to Phitsanulok | Buddhist cosmology (Mount Meru, karma) becomes political doctrine for a Tai king | Coedès 1968 | medium |
| 1351 CE | Ramathibodi I (Uthong) founds Ayutthaya on 4 March; the capital is named after Ayodhya | Sanskrit epic topography and Brahminical kingship transplanted to the Chao Phraya | Baker & Phongpaichit 2017 | high |
| 1351–1359 CE | Ramathibodi I codifies Siamese law; the Thammasat (Dharmasastra) is recast in Pali-Buddhist terms | Manu-derived Dharmaśāstra domesticated: no caste, merit and royal edict override | Baker & Phongpaichit 2016 | medium |
| 1361 CE (or 1331) | Sumana Therā returns from Martaban with twenty monks; Sihala upasampada installed at Wat Pa Mamuang | Sinhala → Sukhothai (Lankavamsa), via Wareru's Mon kingdom | Jinakalamali (1516); Wyatt 2003 | medium |
| 1361/1362 CE | Li Thai is ordained; builds the *Devalayamahaksetra* Brahmin shrine and casts Shiva and Vishnu bronzes | Brahminical and Theravada packages held in one court without merging | Baker & Phongpaichit 2017 | medium |
| 1438 CE | Sukhothai annexed; Ramesuan installed as uparat, ending the independent kingdom | Sukhothai monks and nobles carry the Lankavamsa and court ritual south | Coedès 1968 | high |
| 1455 CE | Borommatrailokkanat's Palatine Law; first Ayutthayan coronation in the 1460s | Brahminical consecration and the *cakravartin* ideal codified at court | Baker & Phongpaichit 2017 | medium |
| 1569–1584 CE | Bayinnaung takes Ayutthaya (1569); Naresuan proclaims independence (1584) | Burmese vassalage disrupts but does not break the Lankan lineage or the Brahmin court | Baker & Phongpaichit 2017 | high |
| 1767 CE | Konbaung army sacks Ayutthaya on 7 April after a fourteen-month siege | Ritual and legal manuscripts destroyed; the model survives at Thonburi and Bangkok | Baker & Phongpaichit 2017 | high |
