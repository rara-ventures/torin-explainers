## What the Sui shu actually says

The Linyi entry in the *Sui shu* 82 opens with its origin story:

> 林邑之先，因漢末交阯女子徵側之亂，內縣功曹子區連殺縣令，自號為王。
> *Linyi's origin: after the late-Han rebellion of Zhengce, a woman of Jiaozhi, the county official's son Khu Lien killed the county magistrate and made himself king.*

Plain reading: the chapter gives no date for the revolt. It places the origin in the late-Han upheaval led by 徵側 (Trưng Trắc in Vietnamese historiography) and names the rebel as Khu Lien. The link between the Trưng rebellion and Khu Lien is the chapter's own framing, not a dated event.

The 605 campaign appears in the same chapter:

> 仁壽末，上遣大將軍劉方為驩州道行軍總管 …… 方入其都，獲其廟主十八枚，皆鑄金為之
> *At the end of the Renshou era the emperor sent General Liu Fang as commander of the Huan prefecture route … Fang entered the capital and took eighteen ancestral images, all cast in gold.*

The chapter also notes that the people "all revere the Buddha, and their writing is the same as India's" (人皆奉佛，文字同於天竺). That is an ethnographic remark. It supports Buddhist practice and Indic script, but it does not record a library or a count of texts.

## Debates & confidence

**1. The 192 CE date (split).** The *Sui shu* gives no year for Khu Lien. Secondary sources disagree: one reference work gives 192 (with 137 as an alternative), and another prefers 137. The Later Han annals are the Tier-1 source and have not been read here. Settling this needs the *Hou Han shu* Nanman chapter. Momoki 1996 and Coedès are cited for this row but neither has been checked. Sa Huynh to Linyi continuity is argued in secondary work (Yamagata 2005, cited in ARI Working Paper 75), not shown by the chapter.

**2. Vo Canh (split).** The early date (2nd–3rd c.) rests on paleography, following Sircar's 1939 survey of earlier opinion. The later date (4th–5th c.) rests on the stele's use of the Vasantatilaka meter, which Sircar and Zakharov connect to Samudragupta-era verse. Zakharov's 2010 note is the main recent argument; its full text is not accessed here. Filliozat 1969 is the standard edition-level study and is not checked page by page. What would settle it: the 1969 article's own palaeographic table, and Zakharov's metrical argument.

**3. Mỹ Sơn E1 (revised).** The 380–413 range belongs to Bhadravarman's earliest inscription and a wooden-era founding, per National Gallery of Art and Britannica summaries. The surviving brick E1 is generally dated to the 7th–8th c. (Arts in SEA database; some secondary summaries). The earlier row's "E1/E4 380–413" conflated the two. What would settle it: a dated study of E1's brick fabric, which I have not located.

**4. The 605 campaign (revised).** The *Sui shu* confirms the expedition, the commander, the capture of the capital, and the eighteen gold images. It does not confirm the 1,350 Buddhist works, the Sanskrit/Pali language of any texts, or the "pre-600 library" inference. The earlier "Suishu 64" citation is wrong: chapter 64 has no Linyi or Liu Fang material, and chapter 53 is the Liu Fang biography. The date "605" is conventional for the Sui emperor's Daye reign; the text says "end of Renshou," which I read as 604–605.

**5. Early Mahayana at Tra Kieu/Thu Bon (removed).** The row cited Schweyer 2011 and "votive tablets" with no checked source. Removed from this note pending a source.
