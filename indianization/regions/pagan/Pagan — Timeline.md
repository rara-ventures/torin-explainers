# Timeline — Pagan (Myanmar)

| Year | Event | Indianization Link | Source | Confidence |
|------|-------|--------------------|--------|------------|
| c. 4th c. CE | Pyu walled cities — Beikthano, Halin, Sri Ksetra — adopt Indian iconography, Brahmic writing and *-varman* royal titles | Indian models arrive through Pyu intermediaries, not direct rule | Stargardt 1990 | medium |
| c. 7th c. CE | Sri Ksetra becomes the premier Pyu city-state, with the richest first-millennium Theravada material in Myanmar | Amaravati-derived stūpa forms and Pali/Sanskrit names set the Buddhist substrate | Stargardt 1990 | medium |
| 832–849 CE | Nanzhao raids weaken the Pyu (750s–830s); Burmans settle the Irrawaddy confluence; Pyinbya fortifies Pagan in 849 (walls radiocarbon-dated c. 980) | Burman kings adopt Pyu Buddhist and Brahmic culture wholesale | Luce 1969; Aung-Thwin 1985 | medium |
| 1044 CE | Anawrahta accedes — the beginning of inscriptionally verifiable Burmese history | Pagan's core is a Pyu–Mon Buddhist ecumene, not a fresh Indian import | Aung-Thwin 1985 | high |
| 1056 CE | Shin Arahan (Mon) converts Anawrahta from Ari Buddhism to Theravada | Thaton Mon → Pagan | Glass Palace Chronicle; Lieberman 2003 | medium |
| 1057 CE | Sack of Thaton — 30 sets of the Pali Canon carried north on 32 white elephants | Thaton → Pagan (Pali Canon) | Kalyani Inscriptions 1476–79; Luce 1969 | medium |
| 1058 CE | Burmese alphabet adapted from the Mon script, one year after the conquest of Thaton | Mon Brahmi → Burmese; Pali liturgy with vernacular donations | Aung-Thwin 1985 | medium |
| 1071 CE | Anawrahta sends monks and scriptures to Lanka, where the Chola invasion had wrecked the sangha | Pagan → Lanka (reverse flow); Pagan as Theravada refuge | Culavamsa; Harvey 1925 | medium |
| c. 1090–1105 CE | Kyanzittha builds the Ananda temple after monks describe the Nandamula cave; the Shwezigon houses the 37 *nats* | Indian rock-cut cave form rebuilt in brick with Mon vaulting; the nat cult absorbed | Luce 1969 | high |
| 1112/13 CE | Myazedi stele: the same dedication in Pyu, Mon, Burmese and Pali | Four scripts and two Buddhist languages in one royal inscription | Luce 1969 | high |
| c. 1153–1186 CE | Parakramabahu link: Pagan sends to the purified Mahavihara (contemporary) | Lanka ↔ Pagan | Culavamsa | medium |
| 1180 CE | Shin Uttarajiva leads a Burmese mission to the Mahāvihāra in Lanka | Lanka → Pagan (Sihala ordination) | Culavamsa; Coedès 1968 | medium |
| 1181 CE | Chapata returns after ten years at the Mahāvihāra with four Sinhalese monks; the Sihala Sangha is installed at Pagan | Lanka → Pagan (Sihala upasampada); Former Order / Latter Order schism | Hmannan Yazawin; Coedès 1968 | medium |
| 13th c. CE | *Dhammasattha* ("dhammathat") first attested in a Pagan inscription — a Pali-Burmese legal genre | Dharmaśāstra translated into Pali and Burmese; local custom and *yazathat* override | Huxley 1997 | medium |
| 1277–1287 CE | Mongol invasions: defeat at Ngasaunggyan (1277), flight and death of Narathihapate (1287), the empire dissolves | Mongol pressure ends the canon-and-monastery state that had modelled itself on Lanka | Yuan shi; Lieberman 2003 | high |
