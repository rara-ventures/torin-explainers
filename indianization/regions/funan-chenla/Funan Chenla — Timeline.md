# Timeline — Funan–Chenla (Mekong Delta)

| Year | Event | Indianization Link | Source | Confidence |
|------|-------|--------------------|--------|------------|
| c. 1st c. CE | Kaundinya–Somā foundation legend: the Brahmin Kaundinya marries the nāga princess Somā and inherits her kingdom — the charter myth of Khmer kingship | Indian Brahmin–local nāga union as a legitimacy formula; absorption, not conquest | Liang shu 54; Coedès 1968 | low |
| c. 1st–2nd c. CE | Oc Eo emerges as a delta entrepôt: Roman medallions, Indian seals and beads, canal link to Angkor Borei | Maritime corridor carrying Indian goods and models before any documented Indian presence | Malleret 1959; Stark 2007 | medium |
| c. 200–300 CE | Amaravati Buddha bronzes at Oc Eo — first Indic cult; mukhalinga at Oc Eo | Ikshvaku Amaravati → Funan via Tamil brokers (Oc Eo entrepôt) | Malleret 1959; Ray 1989 | medium |
| 225–250 CE | Funan sends embassies to Eastern Wu (225–230, 243); the Kang Tai and Zhu Ying mission of c. 245–250 supplies most of the *Liang shu* account | Funan → China relay; the Chinese record is the main evidence for Indian-style kingship | Liang shu 54; Pelliot 1903 | high |
| 357 CE / 5th c. | Chandana sends tamed elephants to the Jin court; Buddhist texts and monks are later relayed Funan → Jiankang | Funan → China (Buddhist relay) | Liang shu 54; Pelliot 1903 | low |
| c. 420 CE | Kaundinya II (Qiáochénrú) is said to have "changed all the laws to conform to the system of India" | Legal Brahmanization: Dharmaśāstra-style court norms adopted, not imposed | Liang shu 54; Pelliot 1903 | low |
| 5th c. CE | Tháp Mười stele: Sanskrit in Pallava-Grantha; Prince Guṇavarman of the Kauṇḍinya line donates to Viṣṇu | Pallava script and the Vaiṣṇava donation formula enter the delta epigraphic corpus | Coedès 1931 | high |
| 514–545 CE | Rudravarman kills his brother Guṇavarman and rules as the last king of Funan | Violent succession inside a Sanskrit-titled, Śaiva–Vaiṣṇava court | Liang shu 54; Coedès 1968 | high |
| 598 CE | Bhavavarman I reigning at Bhavapura; his vassal Narasiṃhagupta sets up a Kalpavāsudeva (Viṣṇu) image at Īśānapura on 13 April 598 | Gupta-style Vaiṣṇava iconography and Sanskrit eulogy in the Chenla heartland | K.600; Coedès 1943 | high |
| 612 CE | Earliest dated Old Khmer inscription, Angkor Borei — Old Khmer cut in the same Pallava-derived script as Sanskrit | Sanskrit script adapted to the vernacular; Sanskrit retained for ritual | Angkor Borei inscription (612); Coedès 1968 | high |
| 616/617 CE | Chenla sends its first embassy to Sui China | Chenla enters the Chinese record as Funan's successor, Indian-derived titulature already in place | Sui shu 82; Pelliot 1903 | high |
| 627 CE | Iśānavarman I cuts K.604 at Īśānapura (Sambor Prei Kuk) on 13 September 627 | Brick temple-mountain, liṅga cult and *-varman* titulature consolidated inland | K.604; Finot 1928 | high |
| c. 627–649 CE | Chenla absorbs Funan: the *Sui shu* credits Citrasena/Mahendravarman, the *Xin Tang shu* credits Iśānavarman I | The Indianized court package passes intact from the delta to the inland polity | Sui shu 82; Coedès 1968 | medium |
| c. 707 CE | Tang histories report the division into Land Chenla (Lùzhēnlà) and Water Chenla (Shuǐzhēnlà) after 706/707 | The Sanskrit court fragments into competing mandalas; Angkor inherits the model | Xin Tang shu 222下; Pelliot 1904 | medium |
| 802 CE | Jayavarman II consecrates the *devaraja* and the centre shifts to Angkor | Chenla's Śaiva kingship ritual becomes the Angkorian state cult | Sdok Kak Thom (1053); Coedès 1968 | medium |
