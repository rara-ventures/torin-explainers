# Angkor (Cambodia)

**802–1431 CE** · Khmer · Sanskrit + Old Khmer · Shaiva → Mahayana → Theravada

The Khmer imperial mandala: a hydraulic state that turned Indian kingship, Sanskrit and temple-mountain cosmology into the largest pre-industrial city on Earth — then converted from Hindu devaraja to Theravada Buddhism without demolishing its gods.

## At a glance

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Polity** | Angkor / Kambuja |
| **Core** | Yasodharapura, Angkor Thom, Angkor Wat (Siem Reap) |
| **Script** | Old Khmer (Pallava-derived), Sanskrit for ritual |
| **State cult** | Shaiva devaraja (802) → Vaishnava (1113) → Mahayana (1181) → Theravada (14th c.) |
| **Indianized?** | Yes — script + kingship + cult + law (≥2 test) |

## Why it matters

Angkor is the clearest case of **Indianization as localization** (Wolters), not colonization: Indian *devaraja*, *cakravartin*, Sanskrit and the temple-mountain form were adopted, but fused with local *nak ta* ancestor spirits, naga cult, and a hydraulic economy of barays and canals. It also shows the **Hindu → Buddhist transition** as retention, not replacement — Vishnu reliefs survive inside a Theravada temple, and Brahmin *bakus* still officiate royal rites.

## Key dates

- **802** — Jayavarman II consecrates the *devaraja* on Phnom Kulen (Sdok Kak Thom K.235); Angkor founded.
- **889–900** — Yasovarman I builds Yasodharapura and the 7.5 km East Baray.
- **1113–1150** — Suryavarman II builds Angkor Wat (162.6 ha, Mt Meru, Vishnu, west-facing).
- **1177** — Cham sack Angkor; Jayavarman VII retaliates.
- **1181–1218** — Jayavarman VII builds Angkor Thom and the Bayon (Mahayana).
- **1296–97** — Zhou Daguan observes Theravada monks and Pali texts alongside existing Hindu rites.
- **c. 1350–1431** — Angkor Wat converts to Theravada; **1431** Ayutthaya sacks Angkor.

## Indian vs local

| Indian import | Khmer localization |
|---|---|
| Devaraja / cakravartin | God-king tied to local *nak ta* and naga ancestry |
| Sanskrit + Pallava script | Old Khmer vernacular for administration and devotion |
| Temple-mountain (Meru) | Baray/canal hydraulic mandala |
| Ramayana / Mahabharata | Bas-relief programs at Angkor Wat and Bayon |
| Brahmin priesthood | Hereditary *bakus* retained after Theravada turn |
