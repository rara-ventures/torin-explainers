# Dvaravati Mon — Mainland Sub-polity

| Year | Event | Indianization Link | Source | Confidence |
|------|-------|--------------------|--------|------------|
| 269–271 CE (coin minted) | Bronze coin of the Roman emperor Victorinus, reportedly found at U Thong | Roman-era maritime exchange reaches the Chao Phraya plain before any Indian religious material; provenance contested | Landes 1982 | low |
| c. 4th–5th century CE | Proto-Dvaravati moated settlements appear at U Thong, Chansen and Si Thep; a Si Thep burial gives 240–390 CE | Indian goods and script enter existing Iron Age exchange networks, not a colonial frontier | Murphy 2016; Sudsawat 2019 | medium |
| c. 5th–7th century CE | Dharmachakra pillars and ye dhammā inscriptions at Nakhon Pathom | The Amaravati/Andhra wheel-and-verse package localised as a civic emblem | Dupont 1959; Brown 1996 | medium |
| c. 550–600 CE | K.978 (Ban Wang Pai) records a Sanskrit inscription in Pallava script naming a king of Si Thep | Earliest Sanskrit epigraphy in Thailand; Indian script used for a local dynastic claim | K.978; Salomon & Revire 2023 | medium |
| late 6th century CE | Two Old Mon votive fragments from Wat Phra Rang, Nakhon Pathom — the earliest written Mon in Thailand | Pali Buddhism transmitted in a Pallava-derived script adapted to Mon | Skilling 2003–04 | medium |
| mid-7th century CE | Xuanzang places To-lo-po-ti between Chenla and Sri Ksetra; Yijing reports reverence for the Three Jewels | External Chinese testimony fixes Dvaravati in the Buddhist ecumene, not a dynastic history | Coedès 1968 | high |
| 7th century CE | Silver medallions at Nakhon Pathom read śrī dvāravatī svarapuṇya in south Indian characters | Sanskrit merit formula on coinage; kingship expressed as puṇya, not genealogy | Boeles 1964; Higham 2014 | medium |
| 7th–8th century CE | K.499 at Si Thep records puṇya in a Brahmanical setting; Viṣṇu and Kṛṣṇa images are produced locally | Vaishnava iconography absorbed alongside Theravada, showing plural religious patronage | Revire 2016; Woodward 2010 | medium |
| c. 750 CE | Haripuñjaya (Lamphun) founded from Lavo; the Camadevi legend dates the foundation to 662 | Mon Buddhist polity carries Dvaravati script, Pali and chedi form into the north | Coedès 1968; Cāmadevivaṃsa | low |
| 8th–9th century CE | Nakhon Pathom and Si Thep at their height; Khu Bua narrative stucco; Khao Khlang Nok stupa (TL 720–875) | Indian iconography rebuilt with local dress, musicians and boats; the stupa form localised | Brown 1996; Woodward 2010; Sudsawat 2019 | medium |
| 859 CE | K.949 dates the reign of Bhagadatta of Śrī Canāśapura | Sanskrit royal genealogy on the Khorat plateau; Indian title used by a Mon polity | K.949; Coedès 1968 | medium |
| 937 CE | Khmer inscription records the princes of Chanasapura from Bhagadatta to Sundaravarman | Khmer epigraphic idiom overlays Mon titulature as Angkorian influence grows | Coedès 1968 | medium |
| 1001–1002 CE | Lavo (Lopburi) falls to Khmer power; a Khmer governor is installed in Rāmaññadeśa | Dvaravati centres subsumed rather than destroyed; Mon script and Buddhism survive | Coedès 1968; Thepthani 1953 | medium |
| 1219 CE | Mon Vat Don inscription (LPh.01) records King Sabbadhisiddhi's ordination at Lamphun | Last great phase of Old Mon epigraphy; Pali Buddhism tied to royal merit | Wyatt 2001 | high |
| 1292 CE | Mangrai besieges and takes Haripuñjaya, ending the last northern Mon polity | Mon script, Pali learning and Theravada institutions pass to Lan Na and Sukhothai | Coedès 1968 | high |
