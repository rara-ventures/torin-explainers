# Timeline — Philippines — The Indianized Periphery

| Year | Event | Indianization Link | Source | Confidence |
|------|-------|--------------------|--------|------|
| c. 500 BCE–200 CE | Austronesian mariners link island Southeast Asia to southern India and Sri Lanka; the Philippines sits on the eastern leg | Trade corridor, no Indian settlement | Bellina 2014; Manguin 2016 | medium |
| 9th–10th c. | Agusan image cast in Mindanao — 2 kg, 21-karat gold female deity, local work from a Javanese (Nganjuk) model | Tantric goldwork; shared visual world | Bosch 1920; Francisco 1963; Capistrano-Baker 2011 | medium |
| 900 (21 Apr) | Laguna Copperplate — Śaka 822; Old Malay in Kawi script; debt of 1 *kati* 8 *suwarṇa* (c. 865 g gold) acquitted at Tundun (Tondo) | Script + legal instrument | Postma 1992 | high |
| 971 / 10th c. | Ma-i first documented in the *Song shi* (971); Butuan sends missions and is given a ruler titled Rajah Kiling | Named and titled in Chinese records, not in its own royal epigraphy | *Song shi*; Go Bon Juan 2005 | medium |
| 9th–12th c. | Butuan Ivory Seal — privy seal inscribed *Butban* in stylised Kawi | Second Sanskrit-script object; a Philippine polity naming itself | National Museum of the Philippines | medium |
| 10th–14th c. | Butuan *balangay* boat-burials at Libertad on the old Agusan river | Maritime polity rather than legend | National Museum; Butuan (historical polity) | high |
| 12th–14th c. | Tabon Caves Garuda gold pendant, Palawan | Vaiṣṇava iconography | National Museum of the Philippines | medium |
| 1225 | Zhao Rugua's *Zhufan zhi* lists Ma-i (Mindoro), Butuan and Sulu as trade polities | Commerce, not kingship | Hirth & Rockhill 1911 | high |
| 13th–14th c. | Kawi and early *suyat* scripts in use; Old Malay serves as the archipelago's trade lingua franca | Script vector, indirect via Java/Malay | Postma 1992 | medium |
| 1365 | *Nagarakretagama* canto 14 lists a dependency read as *Solot* (Sulu) | Contested Majapahit claim | Pigeaud 1960–63 | low |
| 1405–1433 | Zheng He's voyages; Sulu and Butuan embassies to the Ming | Tribute trade; Indic titles persist | *Ming shilu* | medium |
| 1457 | Sulu Sultanate traditionally founded; Islam arrives via Johor, Brunei and Gujarati networks | Islamic layer replaces, but does not erase, Hindu–Buddhist titles | Majul 1973 | low |
| 1521 | Magellan reaches Cebu; Rajah Humabon; Battle of Mactan | Rajah polities still functioning | Pigafetta | high |
| 1565–1571 | Legazpi takes Cebu (1565); Maynila burns in 1570 (Spanish assault or scorched earth — unresolved) and is declared Manila on 24 June 1571; Tondo falls | End of the Indic title layer and of the Tondo–Maynila Chinese-goods duopoly | Spanish chronicles; Maynila (historical polity) | high |
| 1589 | Juan de Plascencia, *Las costumbres de los indios Tagalos* — the near-single source for “barangay” as a label for polities | The pre-colonial order is partly a Spanish reconstruction | Woods; Scott 1994 | medium |
| 1917 | Agusan image found by a Manobo woman on the Wawa River, Esperanza, Agusan del Sur | Modern discovery of the goldwork | Francisco 1963 | high |
| 1922 | Image sold to the Field Museum, Chicago, for ₱4,000; now the subject of a repatriation campaign | Museum history | Field Museum A109928 | high |
