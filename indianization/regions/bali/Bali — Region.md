# Bali (Living Indianization)

Bali is the only Indonesian island where an Indian-derived script, Sanskrit ritual vocabulary and a Shiva-Buddha cult survive. Its Indianization came through Java, and its kings were already using Sanskrit and a Pallava-derived script when Śrī Kesari Warmadewa raised the Belanjong pillar in 914. Coedès called the result an "Indo-Balinese society" that was "independent of Java, used a dialect peculiar to the island, and practiced Buddhism and Sivaism at the same time" (Coedès 1968, 129).

## At a glance

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Polity** | Warmadewa (c. 914–12th c.); Majapahit vassal line (1343–1686); Klungkung paramountcy (1686–1908) |
| **Core area** | Gianyar heartland (Bedulu, Pejeng); later Samprangan, Gelgel, Klungkung |
| **Script** | Old Balinese and Aksara Bali, from Pallava-derived Kawi; Sanskrit and Old Javanese for ritual |
| **State cult** | Śaiva Hinduism fused with Mahayana Buddhism (Siwa-Buddha); Besakih as mother temple |
| **Indianized?** | Yes — script, Sanskrit titulature, varna ideology, temple form, Śaiva priesthood, via Java |

## Why it matters

Bali keeps Indianization from collapsing into a diffusionist story. Elsewhere the Indian elements are read off ruins and Chinese notices; on Bali they are still performed, which makes the mechanism visible but tempts historians to treat the island as a fossil. The sequence also runs through Java: the earliest Indian goods at Sembiran and Pacung (c. 1st century BCE–1st century CE) are trade debris, nearly a millennium before the first Sanskrit inscription (Ardika and Bellwood 1991). The deepest borrowings are ideological rather than technical — irrigation and village federation are Austronesian, kingship and priesthood Indian-Javanese. Coedès's "Indianized" label fits the script and the royal cult; it fits the irrigation economy not at all. Bali is also an archive: the *Nagarakretagama* survives only because Balinese courts copied it (Robson 1995). The Bali Aga of the central mountains, who kept ancestral cults, are an internal control for what "not Indianized" looks like (Reuter 2002).

## Historical arc

**8th century** — Clay *stupikas*, miniature stupas with Buddhist votive tablets, are deposited in Gianyar at Pejeng and Blahbatuh. They are the island's earliest writing, and their bell-shaped profile matches Central Javanese Buddhist art, suggesting transmission through Java rather than a mission from India (Ardika 2009).

**914** — Śrī Kesari Warmadewa issues the Belanjong pillar at Sanur, dated 914 by the Śaka era. It is bilingual, in Old Balinese and Sanskrit scripts, and records campaigns against Gurun and Suwal; the place names are disputed, the claim to Indian-style authority is not (Goris 1954).

**c. 980–1045** — Udayana Warmadewa marries Mahendradatta, an Isyana princess from East Java. Their son Airlangga rules Java (r. 1019–1045) while his brothers Marakata and Anak Wungçu rule Bali; Gunung Kawi's rock-cut *candi* reproduces late Mataram form. Bali enters the Javanese dynastic system without being governed from it.

**11th–13th century** — Kediri (1045–1221) succeeds Airlangga's divided realm in Java, while Bali's kings — Jayasakti (1146–1150), Jayapangus (1178–1181) — issue copper-plate charters. In 1284 Kertanegara of Singhasari attacks Bali and, the *Nagarakretagama* (42.1) says, captures its queen; his fall in 1292 ends Javanese control.

**1343** — Gajah Mada's Majapahit forces defeat Bedulu and install a vassal dynasty under Śrī Aji Kresna Kepakisan at Samprangan. The date comes from the *Babad Dalem*, not a contemporary record; the *Nagarakretagama* (canto 14, 1365) merely lists Bedahulu and Lwa Gajah as dependencies.

**15th–16th century** — As Majapahit fragments, Javanese nobles, priests and artisans move to Bali; the court shifts to Gelgel. Under Dalem Baturenggong, Gelgel claims suzerainty over Lombok and Blambangan, while the Brahmin Nirartha reorganises temple ritual and promotes the *padmasana* cult; Besakih becomes the state temple (Stuart-Fox 2002).

**1686–1908** — Gelgel falls to revolt; Klungkung inherits a ceremonial paramountcy while eight other kingdoms run their own affairs. Dutch campaigns in north Bali (1846–1849) are followed by the Badung *puputan* of 1906 and the Klungkung *puputan* of 18 April 1908.

## Indian vs local

| Indian import | Balinese adaptation |
|---|---|
| Sanskrit *varna* | Recast as *wangsa* descent titles; most Balinese are Sudra, and Ksatria or Wesya status often claims Javanese ancestry, not a division of labour |
| Śaivism and Mahayana Buddhism | Fused as Siwa-Buddha; *pedanda Siwa* and *pedanda Boda* are parallel priestly lines, and Tantric texts like the *Sutasoma* count as ancestral doctrine |
| Temple as the god's house | The *pura* as an open courtyard for congregational worship, with *meru* towers, split gates and a *kaja–kelod* axis |
| Śaiva Siddhanta liturgy | *Subak*: irrigation run by a farmers' assembly from roughly the 9th century, not a state bureaucracy; priests supply the idiom, not the institution (Lansing 2007) |
| Saka calendar | Kept for chronology and auspicious days, interleaved with the native 210-day *Pawukon* cycle |

## Debates & confidence

**Origins:** the 914 Belanjong pillar is the first dated royal inscription, but the 8th-century *stupikas* and the Rsi Markandeya legend in lontar tradition push the start back. The legend is not evidence; the *stupikas* are, and they point to Javanese Buddhist transmission rather than a direct Indian mission (Ardika 2009). **1343:** the conquest is narrated in the 18th-century *Babad Dalem*, not in any contemporary Javanese source; the *Nagarakretagama* lists Bali as a dependency without describing a war, so the date and the "seven months" are traditional. **"Living Indianization":** Picard and Vickers argue that Bali as a preserved Hindu-Javanese museum is a colonial and tourist construction, and that Agama Hindu Dharma was substantially reorganised in the 1950s to satisfy Indonesian state definitions of religion (Picard 1996; Vickers 1989). Confidence: high on epigraphy, medium on political narrative, low on origin legends.
