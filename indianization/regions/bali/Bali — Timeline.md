# Timeline — Bali (Hindu Refuge)

| Year | Event | Indianization Link | Source | Confidence |
|------|-------|--------------------|--------|------------|
| c. 1st century BCE–1st century CE | Rouletted ware, glass beads and Indian pottery at Sembiran and Pacung | Trade contact, not yet Indianization: goods arrive centuries before script or cult | Ardika & Bellwood 1991 | high |
| c. 8th century CE | Buddhist stupikas with votive tablets deposited at Pejeng, Tatiapi and Blahbatuh — the island's earliest writing | Bell-shaped profile matches Central Javanese Buddhist art; transmission via Java, not a direct Indian mission | Ardika 2009 | medium |
| 4 February 914 CE | Śrī Kesari Warmadewa raises the Belanjong pillar at Sanur, bilingual in Old Balinese and Sanskrit | First dated Balinese royal inscription; Nāgarī/Pallava-derived script and Sanskrit titles claim Indian-style authority | Goris 1954; Damais 1959 | high |
| c. 980–1001 CE | Udayana Warmadewa marries Mahendradatta of the East Javanese Isyana dynasty; their son Airlangga is born c. 1001 | Bali enters the Javanese dynastic system through marriage, without being governed from Java | Coedès 1968; Babad Dalem | medium |
| c. 1019–1045 CE | Airlangga rules Java while his brothers Marakata and Anak Wungçu rule Bali; the Gunung Kawi rock-cut candi is cut | Late Mataram temple form reproduced on Balinese soil; the Siwa–Buddha royal cult consolidated | Coedès 1968; Goris 1954 | medium |
| 1178–1181 CE | Jayapangus issues copper-plate charters; tradition makes him marry a Chinese princess, remembered in Barong Landung | Sanskrit–Old Balinese charter formula; royal gift economy modelled on Javanese sīma grants | Goris 1954 | medium |
| 1284 CE | Kertanagara's Pabali expedition defeats Bali and carries off its queen (Nagarakretagama 42.1) | Brief Singhasari overlordship, ending with Kertanagara's fall in 1292 | Nagarakretagama 42.1; Robson 1995 | high |
| 1342–1343 CE | Gajah Mada and Arya Damar land in Bali; after seven months Bedulu falls and Śrī Aji Kresna Kepakisan is installed at Samprangan | Majapahit Siwa–Buddha dyarchy and Javanese lordly titles imposed on the island | Babad Dalem; Babad Arya Tabanan | medium |
| 1365 CE | Nagarakretagama canto 14 lists Bedahulu and Lwa Gajah (Goa Gajah) among Majapahit dependencies | Near-contemporary Javanese confirmation of vassal status, but no campaign narrative | Nagarakretagama 14; Robson 1995 | high |
| 1478 CE | Ranawijaya takes Trowulan and kills Kertabhumi; the Majapahit court splits | Begins the Javanese exodus that will transfer court ritual and texts to Bali | Pararaton; Babad Dalem | medium |
| c. 1492 CE | Dang Hyang Nirartha arrives from Majapahit; promotes the padmasana and the Shaiva pedanda lineage (date contested; often placed under Dalem Baturenggong) | Brahmanical ritual reorganisation; Sanskrit mantra and the lontar canon become the core of Balinese Hinduism | Pringle 2004; Stuart-Fox 2002 | low |
| 1527 CE | Demak destroys Daha; Hindu nobles, priests and artisans flee to Bali | Bali becomes the archive of Indo-Javanese culture; the Nagarakretagama survives only in Balinese copies | Ricklefs 2008; Robson 1995 | medium |
| 1686 CE | Klungkung replaces Gelgel as royal seat; Bali fragments into nine kingdoms under the Dewa Agung's ceremonial paramountcy | Indian-derived title and sacred regalia persist without a unified state | Babad Dalem; Hanna 2004 | medium |
| 1906–1908 CE | Dutch take Badung (1906) and Klungkung (18 April 1908) after puputan; royal rule ends | Colonial conquest dismantles royal patronage, not village temple or subak institutions | Hanna 2004; Picard 2004 | high |
| 14 June 1958 CE | Balinese Hindus petition for recognition of Agama Hindu Dharma, defining Sang Hyang Widhi Wasa as the one supreme god | Indian theology recast to satisfy the Indonesian state's monotheism requirement; "living Indianization" becomes a legal category | Picard 2004; Ramstedt 2003 | high |
