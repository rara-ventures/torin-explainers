# Timeline — 08 8Th Century

| Year | Event | Region | Source | Confidence |
|------|-------|--------|--------|------------|
| 706/707 CE | **Chenla splits into Land (Lu Zhenla) and Water (Shui Zhenla) Chenla** after the Tang *shenlong* era — a Chinese administrative label for what was already a patchwork of principalities, not a witnessed partition | Cambodia | *Xin Tangshu*; Pelliot 1904 | medium |
| 713 CE | Queen Jayadevī's K.904 inscription at the Western Baray laments "bad times" — the last dated pre-Angkorian royal charter from the Angkor region | Angkor | K.904; Vickery 1998 | high |
| 717–723 CE | Land Chenla opens direct embassies to the Tang court (717, again 750, 771, 799) and backs Mai Thúc Loan's revolt against Chinese Annam (722–723) — a mainland polity using Indian-derived kingship and Chinese diplomacy at once | Cambodia, Vietnam | *Xin Tangshu*; Pelliot 1904 | medium |
| 732 CE | **Canggal (Gunung Wukir), 654 Śaka — Sanjaya erects a Śivaliṅga in Kunjarakunja and claims rule over Yava, "rich in grain and gold"; Sanskrit verse in Pallava script** — the Śaiva baseline against which the Buddhist Śailendras are read | Central Java | Canggal inscription; Damais 1952 | high |
| c. 750–800 CE | **Dvāravatī florescence** — moated Mon cities (Nakhon Pathom, U Thong, Si Thep, Khu Bua), Dharmacakra "wheels of the law", Mon Buddha types; the densest Buddhist archaeology in mainland Southeast Asia | Central Thailand | Brown 1996; Murphy 2016 | medium |
| c. 770–780 CE | **Borobudur begun** by the Śailendras on a natural hill above the Kedu plain — five construction stages, c. 780 on Dumarçay's sequence or 760/770 on Miksic's, and not finished until c. 825–833 | Central Java | Dumarçay 1991; Miksic 1990 | medium |
| 774 CE | Cham tradition: seaborne raiders — C.38 calls them "men living on food more horrible than cadavers" — carry off the jewelled mukhaliṅga of Po Nagar; the 774 date is traditional, the stele itself is 781 | Champa (Kauthara) | C.38; Maspero 2002 | low |
| 775 CE | **Ligor B (Vat Sema Muang), Kawi face — a Śailendra mahārāja, usually identified as Panangkaran, is proclaimed on the Kra isthmus**; the older Ligor A face hails a Srivijayan "king of kings" | Malay Peninsula | Coedès 1959; Zakharov 2012 | medium |
| 778 CE | **Kalasan, 700 Śaka — Mahārāja Panangkaran, urged by his guru "jewel of the Śailendra family", builds the Tārābhavana for Ārya Tārā plus a vihāra, and endows Kalasa village to the saṅgha**; first dated use of the dynastic name, in Prāṇāgarī script | Central Java | Kalasan inscription; Coedès 1968 | high |
| 781–784 CE | Satyavarman recovers Kauthara and restores Po Nagar — stele C.38 in 781, reinstallation of the mukhaliṅga in 784; the indigenous goddess Bhagavatī begins to eclipse the liṅga as the sanctuary's principal cult | Champa | C.38; Ngô Văn Doanh 2006 | high |
| 782 CE | **Kelurak, 704 Śaka — Mañjuśrī sanctuary (Candi Sewu) for King Indra/Dharaṇindra, with Buddha-Dharma-Saṅgha equated to Brahmā-Viṣṇu-Maheśvara and a guru from Gauḍa (Bengal) presiding**; the Vajrayāna turn and the Bengal link, though the teacher's name rests on a damaged text | Central Java | Kelurak inscription; Coedès 1968 | medium |
| c. 790 CE | **A Javanese (Śailendra) fleet attacks Water Chenla; Kumbhupura is sacked and the last Water Chenla king killed** — the trauma behind Jayavarman II's later claim to have freed Kambujadeśa "from Java"; the location of that "Java" remains disputed | Cambodia | Coedès 1968; Vickery 1998 | low |
| 792 CE | **Mañjuśrīgrha inscription, 714 Śaka (2 November 792) — Old Malay verses record a Vajrāsana prasāda for Mañjuśrī at Sewu**; the oldest known verse text in Malay, a local poet working in an Indian metre | Central Java | Griffiths 2020 | high |
| 802 CE | **Jayavarman II consecrated chakravartin on Phnom Kulen (Mahendraparvata) by the Brahmin Hiraṇyadāma, installing the *kamrateṅ jagat ta rāja*, "lord of the world who is king"** — Angkor's founding rite, known only from K.235 (1053), whose "came from Java" is contested | Cambodia | K.235; Coedès 1968 | medium |
| 817 CE | Senapati Par, general of Harivarman I, makes endowments at Po Nagar and mounts attacks on Khmer territory — Cham military reach in the generation of Jayavarman II | Champa | Coedès 1968 | medium |
