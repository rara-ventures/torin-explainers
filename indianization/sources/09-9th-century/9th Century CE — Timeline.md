# Timeline — 09 9Th Century

| Year | Event | Region | Source | Confidence |
|------|-------|--------|--------|------------|
| 824 CE | **Karangtengah (Kayumwungan), 746 Śaka — bilingual charter: the Sanskrit half names Samaratungga and Pramodhawardhani's Jinalaya (Borobudur); the Old Javanese half records tax-free rice land at Kayumwungan** — Sanskrit for doctrine, vernacular for land | Central Java | Karangtengah inscription; de Casparis 1956 | high |
| c. 825 CE | **Borobudur completed** — the Jinalaya of the 824 charter, its original name perhaps Bhūmisambhāra; Munoz puts completion at 825, Dumarçay's fifth stage at c. 833, and no foundation charter survives | Central Java | de Casparis 1956; Dumarçay 1991 | medium |
| 842 CE | **Tri Tepusan — Pramodhawardhani grants tax-free *sima* land to maintain the *kamūlān* Bhūmisambhāra**, generally read as Borobudur's endowment charter and the best evidence for its cult | Central Java | Tri Tepusan inscription; de Casparis 1956 | high |
| c. 850 CE | **Prambanan (Śivagrha) begun by Rakai Pikatan** — a 47 m Śiva temple within a 240-structure complex with a Rāmāyaṇa gallery; a red-paint graffito reading "pikatan" survives on a balustrade finial | Central Java | Shivagrha inscription; Jordaan 1993 | medium |
| 856 CE | **Śivagrha inscription, 778 Śaka — Pikatan defeats a rebel fortified at Ratu Boko (later read as Balaputra), abdicates, and Dyah Lokapāla dedicates the temple; the Śiva image is inaugurated on 12 November 856 and the Opak river is diverted** | Central Java | Shivagrha inscription; de Casparis 1956 | high |
| c. 860 CE | **Nalanda copper plate of Devapala, year 39 — Balaputra, Śailendra king of Suvarṇadvīpa, endows five villages (Nandivanāka, Maṇivāṭaka, Naṭikā, Hasti, Pālāmaka) for a monastery at Nalanda**; the strongest document of a Java–Sumatra–Bengal Buddhist circuit | Bengal, Sumatra | Nalanda plate; Sankalia 1934 | medium |
| 875 CE | **Đồng Dương (Indrapura), 13 May 875 — Indravarman II founds the Mahāyāna vihāra of Lakṣmīndralokeśvara with a royal Avalokiteśvara cult, taking the posthumous name Paramabuddhaloka**; Buddhism is the Cham court religion until the Vietnamese sack of 982 | Champa | C.66; Maspero 2002 | high |
| 877/878 CE | Indravarman I succeeds Jayavarman III at Hariharalaya and begins a three-part programme: the Indratāṭaka reservoir (3.8 by 0.8 km), the ancestor temple, and the state temple-mountain | Angkor | Coedès 1968; Higham 2001 | high |
| 879/880 CE | **Preah Ko consecrated, 25 January 880 — six brick towers for Jayavarman II (Parameśvara), Rudravarman and Pṛthivīndravarman with their queens**; the devaraja fused with ancestor worship in brick and sandstone | Angkor | K.713; Bhattacharya 2009 | high |
| 881 CE | **Bakong, K.826 — the first fully developed Khmer temple-mountain: five sandstone tiers, eight brick towers, double moats, nāga causeway**; Meru in stone, its gateways and stairways often compared with Borobudur | Angkor | K.826; Dumarçay & Royère 2001 | high |
| 889 CE | **Yasovarman I takes the throne after a succession war with his brother and repels a Cham invasion** (Cham sources credit Indravarman II with the repulse); in his first year he founds about a hundred *āśramas* across the kingdom | Angkor, Champa | Coedès 1968; Maspero 2002 | medium |
| 889–900 CE | **Yasodharapura founded around Phnom Bakheng, with the East Baray (Yaśodharatataka) at 7.5 by 1.8 km and hilltop temples at Phnom Krom and Phnom Bok** — the hydraulic-mandala capital that lasts six centuries | Angkor | Coedès 1968; Higham 2001 | high |
| 893 CE | **Lolei, K.324 (8 July 893) — the Indratāṭaka is completed and Lolei raised on an island within it, dedicated to Indravarman I and his ancestors**; the last of the Roluos temples | Angkor | K.324; Glaize 1993 | high |
| 900 CE | **Laguna Copperplate, 21 April 900 (Śaka 822) — Old Malay in Kawi script from Luzon: the debt of Namwaran is cleared before the chief of Tundun (Tondo), with Medang invoked as an authority**; the earliest dated document in the Philippines, showing how far the Sanskritic diplomatic idiom travelled | Philippines | Postma 1992 | high |
