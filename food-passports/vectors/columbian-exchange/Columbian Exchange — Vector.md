# Vector: Columbian Exchange — The Ships (Atlantic + Pacific legs)

> Term coined by Alfred Crosby 1972. Mechanism: **biological transfer by sail**, both directions, with the tropics as sorting table. The only "vector" in the lab with no human intention behind the menu — a feedback loop of crops, livestock, diseases, and later, people.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Mechanism** | Seed chests, ship gardens, ballast, enslaved provisioners; then plantation & rail amplification |
| **Carriers** | Conquistadors, missionaries, merchants, enslaved & indentured laborers |
| **Period** | 1492–1800 core; W5 industrial phase completes the circle |
| **Two legs** | Atlantic (Seville/Lisbon ↔ Caribbean/Brazil) + Pacific (Acapulco ↔ Manila, the *galleon door*) |
| **What else crossed** | Smallpox/measles west→east kill ~90% of some populations; wheat/cattle/sugar east→west reorganize the Americas; syphilis? contested |

## Why it's the spine vector
Every passport's first stamp is on a ship. The Pacific leg explains Asia's *two doors* (see traces). Crosby's insight — ecology moves faster than culture — is why the crops naturalized *before* anyone decided to adopt them.
