# Wave 3 — Peasants' Gardens (1650–1800)

> The age states *beg* and peasants *decide*. Potato promotion by royal edicts (Frederick the Great's *Kartoffelbefehl* 1756 — real text, exaggerated legend — *verify*), Parmentier's PR showmanship 1770s France; maize + peanut pair into West Africa's new protein-carb system; sweet potato feeds Qing China's marginal lands as population doubles; and in Naples 1692, Antonio Latini prints the first tomato-sauce recipe and — insult to the modern brand — credits it *alla spagnuola*, "the Spanish way." Elite kitchens reject the immigrant; the poor eat it anyway.

## Story list
1. Prussia/Silesia: soldiers & potatoes (Seven Years' War rations) *verify*.
2. Languedoc & Basque coast: maize as *millo* — the "Turkish grain" naming era; pellagra appears with maize monoculture (1730s Italy *verify*).
3. Latini 1692 + early 1700s rice-with-tomato recipes; China: 1719 gazetteer — "now eaten in every kitchen" — *verify*.
4. West African groundnut belt forms (1700s); maize rides the same paths inland.
5. Kangxi-era records: maize & sweet potato on highland "mountain farms" as refugee crops of the frontier poor (*verify*).

## Passport angle
Wave 3 is the **adoption inversion**: the crop becomes food for people nobody asked. National cuisines will later be built on what peasants ate against advice.
