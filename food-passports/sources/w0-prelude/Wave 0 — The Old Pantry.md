# Wave 0 — The Old Pantry (before 1492)

> The pre-exchange baseline — so we can prove the "national" foods that *look* ancient often aren't, and that food already traveled before 1492. All the world's heat was **pepper**: Piper nigrum (India), long pepper/pippali, timur & ji-tima (Nepal), Sichuan pepper (China), mustard & ginger everywhere. The Americas owned none of it — and owned *Capsicum*, waiting.

## Story list
1. Wheat's 4,000-year westward crawl (Fertile Crescent → Mediterranean → China): noodles-vs-pasta debate, treat skeptically.
2. Sugarcane: New Guinea → India's crystallization tech (khanda → "candy") → Islamic agriculture west.
3. Austronesians carry banana & coconut across the Indian Ocean — plantain Uganda by first millennium.
4. Rice's two independent homes (Asian O. sativa, African O. glaberrima — Niger delta).
5. Spice monsoons: pepper was so valuable it renamed itself "pippali vs marich" in Sanskrit; the pre-Columbian spice trade is why Vasco da Gama had a map.

## Passport angle
W0 is the **control wave**: every "native" staple below this line was itself an immigrant once. Run `thesis.md` test: banana-Uganda and sugarcane-India pass; wheat-China is transit; pepper-India is native.
