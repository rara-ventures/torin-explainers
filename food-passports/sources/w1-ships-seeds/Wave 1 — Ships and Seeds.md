# Wave 1 — Ships and Seeds (1493–1550)

> The exchange opens: Columbus's second voyage (1493) carries wheat, sugarcane cuttings, and seeds west-to-east and back; Vasco da Gama (1498) hands the chili a Portuguese passport east. Within 60 years the Americas' crops are botanical curiosities from Seville to Lisbon, and the "pepper" Columbus misnamed is already being grown in Goan gardens — as ornamental, then as poor man's spice.

## Story list
1. 1493: Hispaniola plantings — first New World wheat & sugar (dates *verify*).
2. 1498–1505: Portuguese India — chilies and pineapples noted in Goa/Coimbatore accounts (*verify* — Garcia? *Alier*?).
3. 1544: Europe's tomato — Fuchs's *pomodoro* described; nobody eats it.
4. The papal/botanical naming scramble: "Indian corn," "Turkish grain," "golden apple" — every name wrong, every name a route.
5. Reverse wave 1: syphilis vs maize — biological exchange cuts both ways (Crosby's frame).

## Passport angle
Wave 1 stamps are *arrivals without adoption* — the visa, not the citizenship. Compare with W3/W4 where the same crops get eaten.
