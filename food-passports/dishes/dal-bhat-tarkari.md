# Dish: Dal Bhat Tarkari (Nepal)

**The receipt:** rice + dal (both genuinely local, ancient — the honest half of the plate), *tarkari* of aloo/tomato/cauliflower/cabbage (all New World or colonial-era imports — *verify* cauliflower/cabbage = Old World temperate crops, Portuguese/British era arrival), *chiuri ko achar* or aloo achar (chili — 16th-c. Amazonian; timur displaced), *kcho ka achar* (papaya, Brazil→Goa→Kathmandu), *simi/sanako* (common bean, Americas). The bhat-dal half could feed your great-great-grandparents; **the tarkari half couldn't**.

| Component | Born | Arrived Nepal | "Traditional" since |
|---|---|---|---|
| bhat + gundri dal | local | always | always |
| aloo tarkari | Andes | c. 1800s (mid-hills, via British India — *verify*) | ~3 generations |
| chiuri + tomato achar | Mexico | 18–19c. | everyday |
| timur (pre-chili heat) | Himalaya | ancient | *now* the heritage relabel |

**Script hook:** "Nepal's national meal is half ancient, half Columbian Exchange — and nobody told the grandmothers."
