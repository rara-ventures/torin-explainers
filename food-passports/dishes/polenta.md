# Dish: Polenta (Veneto/Lombardy)

**The receipt:** maize meal + water/salt — and Italians named it *grano turco* ("Turkish grain", via Ottoman/Iberian routes — the passport lie is in the name ✔). Ancient polenta was **farro**, then **buckwheat/chestnut** (*verify* chestnut-flour polenta of the Liguria mountains). Maize arrived 16c. as poultry feed, became the food of the Veneto poor by the 1600s–1700s (the "polenta-eater" insult from Tuscany proves adoption), and now sells as rustic Italian heritage — while *polenta* in local dialect still refers to the ancient grain mush, so the dish kept its name after swapping its body. Pellagra outbreaks (maize-only diets, 18th–19c. Italy — *verify* date range) are the immigrant's criminal record.

**Script hook:** "Veneto's 'ancient' polenta is 400 years old, named after Turkey, made from Mexican grass, and once killed people — which is exactly how new staple foods graduate."
