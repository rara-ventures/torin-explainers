# Dish: Ugali (Kenya/Tanzania)

**The receipt:** maize flour + water — one ingredient, and it's from Mexico. Pre-ugali East Africa ran on sorghum and finger-millet porridges, and the Swahili word *ugali* itself first named a sorghum porridge (*verify dating via food-history literature, e.g. Pilcher 2006*). Portuguese carried maize to Mozambique 16c.; it climbed the slave/ivory roads, and by the 1800s–colonial era maize replaced sorghum across the plateau — **a staple swap in two centuries, invisible because maize now has no foreign accent** (Kenyan "amahindi"? that word marks it Indian-origin — *verify naming strata*).

**Script hook:** "Kenya's word for 'food' is a Mexican grass. The sorghum it replaced still survives only in beer and ceremony."
