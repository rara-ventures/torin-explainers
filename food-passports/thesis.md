# Thesis — What Counts as a Naturalized Food

> **Bright-line rule:** A crop is **naturalized** in a kitchen iff it meets **≥2 of**:
> 1. **Unmarked use** — recipes assume it without explanation (tarkari *means* potato in many hill dialects' usage; "achar" assumes aloo/papaya)
> 2. **National branding** — the country sells it as its own (Hungarian paprika, Italian tomato, Korean red kimchi)
> 3. **Festival/ritual adoption** — identity events built on it (Hemja National Potato Festival; La Tomatina; Szeged paprika harvest days — *verify*)
> 4. **Pre-immigrant competitor displaced** — timur/ji-tima, pippali, long pepper, white kimchi, millet/sorghum ugali, chestnut-farro polenta

Trade alone (a crop eaten only by colonists) is **not** naturalization.

## Mainstay vs Transit (the core/relay analogue)

| Class | Test | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Citizen (mainstay: true)** | meets ≥2 criteria | tomato-Italy, potato-Nepal, chili-Sichuan/Korea/Hungary/Thailand, cassava-Congo, maize-Kenya (ugali), groundnut-Nigeria |
| **Transit labor (mainstay: false)** | grown for export, not eaten locally | cacao-West Africa (produces 60%+, barely eats chocolate), vanilla-Madagascar, pineapple-Philippines/Costa Rica, rubber—not food, cashew-India counterexample: adopted hard |
| **Reverse immigrants** | Old World crops naturalized in the Americas | wheat-Argentina (pasta nation), coffee-Brazil/Colombia, sugarcane-Caribbean, banana-Caribbean/Ecuador |

## Why this lab exists

`indianization/` proved ideas traveled without conquest. This lab proves the same for **food** — and food is the stronger evidence because it enters the body and the calendar. The Indianization vault's `indianized:` checkbox becomes `mainstay:` here; the dish notes are our inscriptions.
