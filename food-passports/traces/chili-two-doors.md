# Trace: Chili — Two Doors into Asia (+ One Back Door)

## Route
**Amazon-Orinoco (W0: C. frutescens) / Mexico (C. annuum) → Lisbon (1493 "pepper") → DOOR 1: Goa, c. 1500 (Estado da Índia) → Kerala, Tamil country, Burma, Tibet?, Nepal via plains trade → DOOR 2: Acapulco→Manila 1571+ (Columbian Exchange Pacific leg) → Fujian/Zhejiang gazetteers 1591, Sichuan 1749-era, Korea via Tsushima/China, Japan Nanban 1542+ → BACK DOOR: Ottoman "Turkish pepper" → Balkans → Hungary paprika.**

## The substitution map (each kitchen's before/after)
| Kitchen | Before chili | After | When "naturalized" |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kerala/India | Piper nigrum, pippali | "chili cheaper than pepper" — the crop that broke the spice monopoly | by 1600s (*verify*) |
| Nepal | timur (Zanthoxylum), ji-tima (Korean pepper), wild pepper | chiuri everyday; timur demoted to "heritage" label | ~2 generations |
| Sichuan | Sichuan pepper (solo *ma*), mustard, ginger, zhuyu | mala duo; doubanjiang standard | 18th–19th c. |
| Korea | salt-fermented white kimchi, mustard | red gochugaru kimchi | 18th–19th c. (*verify*) |
| Thailand | rhizomes, pepper, sour-ferments | prik kee noo everywhere | 17th–18th c. |
| Hungary | black pepper, caraway | paprika *the* national spice | 19th c. |

## Script beats
1. Hook: "One plant walked into Asia through two different doors and rewrote seven cuisines."
2. Map animation: Goa door + Manila door + Ottoman back door.

4. Twist: Columbus named it wrong ("pepper") — and the wrong name IS the evidence.
