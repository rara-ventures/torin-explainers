# S14: The minister said carbonara is from ancient Rome. It's from 1944.

**The assumption:** carbonara = Roman eternity (an Italian agriculture minister said exactly this in 2023 ⚠ — and the internet ate it).

**The reveal:** no tomato, so people call it "pre-Columbian authentic" — wrong anyway. First documented *spaghetti alla carbonara* is mid-20th c.; strongest origin stories: Rome, 1944–45, **US rations** — soldiers' breakfast (bacon + eggs) met pasta; "spaghetti and eggs" in liberated Rome was GI-canteen food. The charcoal-burners (*carbonari*) etymology is folk romance ⚠.

**Proof stamps:** 1944–45 Allied Rome rations ⚠ → first recipes postwar ⚠ → 1950s restaurant canon → 2023 minister-gaffe viral.

**Kicker:** Italy's "proof we had cuisine before Columbus" is a WWII military ration dish — the same war that fed us Spam (S28) and ketchup-tech (S10). Occupation food becomes heritage within one generation. Every "we've always eaten this" has a *since* — and you can usually date it to a war.
