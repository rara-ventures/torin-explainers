# S1: Italian cuisine without tomatoes (it existed — for 3,000 years)

**The assumption:** "Can you imagine Italy without tomatoes?" People answer: no. That's the hook — the answer is yes, easily, and Rome never had one.

**The reveal:** Tomato sat in Italian gardens as a shiny ornamental for ~150 years after arriving (1544 herbals, "golden apple"). Neapolitan poor started cooking it in the late 1700s. Pizza with tomato = 1800s street food. "Ancient Italian cuisine" before that: butter, lard, cheese, herbs, broth, offal, sugar-spice mixes.

**Proof stamps:** 1544 Fuchs (ornamental) → 1692 Latini recipe, credited *alla spagnuola* ⚠verify wording → 1830s pizzerias → 1889 Margherita (folklore) → DOP "Made in Italy" era.

**Kicker:** The tomato didn't join Italian cuisine. It *is* Italian cuisine — after the cuisine stopped being what it was. That's not corruption; that's how every "tradition" works.

**Twist on the twist:** the wheat under the pizza is often Canadian durum now ⚠ — passport check again.
