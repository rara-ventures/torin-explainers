# S6: East Africa's staple — a 400-year-old immigrant that fired the natives

**The assumption:** Ugali = African food, as old as the continent.

**The reveal:** Ugali was **sorghum/millet porridge** for most of East African history; maize came with the Portuguese (1500s coast), climbed inland through slave/ivory roads (provisioning crop), and became "the staple" mainly in the **1800s–1900s** ⚠. The native grains weren't wrong — they were rehired as beer and ceremony only. Even Kenya's word for maize, *amahindi*, literally says "from India" — every language keeps the route on the label.

**Proof stamps:** 1500s Mozambique coast ⚠ → 1600s–1700s inland diffusion (ahead of any state) → 1800s plateau adoption → colonial maize politics → ugali = "food" unmarked.

**Kicker:** The fastest naturalization in the lab: a Mexican grass became the Swahili word for *the meal itself* — and sorghum, the old king, kept only the beer.
