# S22: The white gazpacho proves it: Iberia's "ancient" red soups post-date Columbus

**The assumption:** gazpacho and ratatouille = peasant Mediterranean since antiquity.

**The reveal:** Andalusia still has **ajo blanco — white gazpacho**: bread, almonds, garlic, oil, vinegar. *That* is the pre-Columbian soup — the surviving fossil. The **red** gazpacho needs the tomato (19th-c. standardization ⚠). Same in Provence: ratatouille is tomato + zucchini + bell pepper — three Americas in one pot, a dish first documented in the **20th c.** (1930s codification, tourism-era — ⚠). The "ancient peasant stew" is a *post-Columbian* creation with a *pre-Columbian* cousin still on the menu — the white one is the control group.

**Proof stamps:** pre-1500 almond-garlic soups ✔ → 1544 tomato ornamental → 1800s red gazpacho print ⚠ → 1930s ratatouille canon ⚠ → 1970s Provence tourism globalizes it.

**Kicker:** keep the white version on screen and the argument wins itself: "Which gazpacho is older? The one without the New World." Pair with S01 (tomato-Italy) and S05 (grano-turco) for a "Mediterranean red lie" triptych.
