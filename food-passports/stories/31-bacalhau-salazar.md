# S31: Portugal's national dish is Norwegian cod, and Salazar's state put it there

**The assumption:** bacalhau = Portuguese identity since the Age of Discoveries ("365 ways to cook it!").

**The reveal:** stockfish/salt-cod was a **Basque/British/Norse northern-seas trade product**; Portugal *bought* it from Norway (Lofoten exports to Iberia run on steam + rail tech, 19th c. ⚠). Its explosion into "national cuisine" is **1930s–40s political**: Salazar's Estado Novo promoted cod as the *people's meat* in a protein-poor, empire-building diet — state propaganda + cheap supply (Norway's modern fisheries) + Catholic fasting culture made it daily food. The "conquest-era sailors' ration" backstory is a **retrofit myth**. Cod today = heritage; the actual story = the first mass-food *global supply chain* (Norway→Lisbon) dressed in imperial clothes.

**Proof stamps:** 1400s Basque Grand Banks fishing ⚠ → 1800s Norwegian steam exports to Portugal ⚠ → 1936–46 Estado Novo cod campaigns ⚠ → 1960s canned "bacalhau" era → 2000s price spikes reveal dependency again.

**Kicker:** the dish that proves Portugal "always ate the sea" is a state-directed import from the North Atlantic — and when cod prices tripled (2010s ⚠), the heritage revealed its supply chain again. Compare S27: Brazil invented the *origin story*; Portugal was handed the *product* and wrote a story to match.
