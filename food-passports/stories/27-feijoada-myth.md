# S27: Feijoada's famous origin story — the slaves' pot-au-feu leftovers — is a myth. And the myth is the interesting part.

**The assumption:** black Brazil knows: feijoada = the "leftovers" dish enslaved people made from masters' discarded pork parts.

**The reveal:** food historians (Campos Júnior and others ⚠) trace it to **Portuguese *cozido*** — one-pot beans-and-meat stews — and the "slave leftovers" narrative appears in print only in the **20th c.**, as modernist nationalists *invented* a racialized origin story for a dish that was just colonial stew upgraded by wealth (the real "leftovers" diet of enslaved Brazilians was *angu* cornmeal + okra, scholars note ⚠). Pork-trotter pride = a literary invention of the 1930s–40s *cozinha nacional* project.

**Proof stamps:** 1500s Portuguese cozido ✔ → 1800s "feijoada" elite restaurants (not slave quarters!) ⚠ → 1930s nationalist canonization → 1940s samba/poem era (feijoada as racial heritage) → today UNESCO-era plate politics.

**Kicker:** the deepest-level version of this lab's thesis: not just the *dish* is an immigrant — the *origin story* is an immigrant too. When a nation needs a people's history, it sometimes cooks one. Compare S31: a dictatorship literally legislated the national dish.
