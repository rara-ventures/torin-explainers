# S9: Africa grows the chocolate — and doesn't eat it

**The assumption:** Chocolate = Europe's luxury (Swiss/Belgian brand), cacao = "tropical" commodity.

**The reveal:** Cacao is Amazonian, made into a frothed elite drink by Maya/Aztecs (the only ones who "ate" it for 2,000 years). Europe added sugar (1600s) then milk (1875 ⚠ Peter) and built the brand. Then the **production moved to West Africa** (São Tomé → Gold Coast → Côte d'Ivoire, 1880s–1900s ⚠) with a brutal logic: smallholder + plantation labor growing a crop almost no one in the producing country consumes. Today Africa grows ~60% of the world's beans; per-capita chocolate consumption in Côte d'Ivoire is a rounding error vs Switzerland's.

**Kicker:** The chocolate bar's passport: born in the Amazon, married sugar in India, got a European face, works in African fields. Nobody in the supply chain is where the story starts — the most complete "transit-labor" case in the vault.
