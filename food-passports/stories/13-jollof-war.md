# S13: The jollof war is Nigeria vs Ghana arguing over a Mexican tomato

**The assumption:** The best jollof is *ours* (and the dish is ancient Wolof royal heritage).

**The reveal:** Wolof/Jolof *thiéboudienne* is the ancestor — and **Africa has its own rice** (*Oryza glaberrima*, Niger delta, ~1,000 BCE ✔✔). But the dish's red engine — tomato + scotch bonnet + onion paste — is Columbian: tomato (Mexico), capsicum (Amazon), both via Portuguese coastal contact; the "tomato-stew base" economy only standardizes in the **19th–20th century** with tinned/paste imports ⚠. The modern jollof war itself is a **broadcast-era** phenomenon (radio/TV rivalries; contest culture). So: two nations, one genuinely African grain, one genuinely ancient technique — and an argument whose central ingredient is an immigrant that arrived after both countries' names for it existed.

**Kicker:** The most passionate food fight on Earth is 40% a fight about the Columbian Exchange wearing a flag.

**Verify:** jollof name-dating (1960s pan-African era?), tomato base arrival century in Senegambia.
