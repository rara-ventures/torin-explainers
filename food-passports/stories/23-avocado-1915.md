# S23: Avocado toast is 100 years old — and the fruit changed its name because people laughed

**The assumption:** avocado = ancient superfood; toast trend = modern wellness.

**The reveal:** the Aztec *ahuacatl* fruit reached the US as the **"alligator pear"** — and its Nahuatl-derived name had embarrassing associations (testicle etymology ⚠; the word's joke history is real marketing lore), so California growers **rebranded it "avocado" in the 1915–20s Panama-Pacific fair era ⚠**, then hit a 1920s-30s land boom ("green gold" — a speculative rush California still remembers). The Hass mutation was patented 1935 ⚠. Toast-as-trend: **1990s–2010s Australian/US café culture**; global demand then re-exported the boom south — Michoacán deforestation, cartel taxation, "green blood" ecology (⚠ stats). Mexico's own cuisine had the fruit 9,000 years; it took America a century to make it a *brand*, then the brand wrecked the region that grew it.

**Proof stamps:** 8000 BCE Peru/Mexico gourd-fossil archaeology ⚠ → 1850s California trial plantings ⚠ → 1915/20s name rebrand ⚠ → 1935 Hass patent ⚠ → 2010s toast wave → 2020s Michoacán crisis.

**Kicker:** the exchange runs full circle now — a crop that went Americas→world is coming back as demand *into* the Americas, and the origin country pays the ecological tax for a word a marketing committee picked. S33 flips the same cycle at home in Nepal (kodo → superfood). Pair: this is "superfood" as a *late* naturalization wave.
