# S32: French fries: Belgian argument, Andean tuber, American industrialization — and India got a *different fry* because of cows

**The assumption:** French fries = France (or America's fast-food invention).

**The reveal:** the fry is **Belgian/French coal-mine street logic** (cut potatoes fried against winter hunger — the "American soldier in Belgium called them French ⚠" origin of the name is lore); **Russet Burbank** + Idaho irrigation + rail refrigeration industrialized it ⚠; Simplot's **frozen fry (1950s–60s)** made McDonald's the fry economy it is ⚠ — the fry is the potato's *W5* chapter (see [[07-potato-festival]]'s Hemja line). Then the plot India wrote: when McDonald's opened (1990s ⚠), the **beef-tallow fry** sparked Hindu & Jain lawsuits (settled 2001–02 ⚠) — US fries still carry "natural beef flavoring"; India got the **vegetable-oil fry + McAloo Tikki** (the Columbian Exchange eating itself: a potato burger for the potato's newest citizens). Nepali street *fries* and *wai-wai* seasoning (W5 + S24's MSG chain) are the Kathmandu leg.

**Proof stamps:** 1800s Meuse fries ⚠ → 1900s Idaho Russet → 1953–67 Simplot frozen deals ⚠ → 1992–2002 tallow lawsuits ⚠ → 2010s Nepal fries-at-tea-stall culture.
