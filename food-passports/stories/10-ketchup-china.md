# S10: "Ketchup" is a Chinese word for fish sauce

**The assumption:** Ketchup = Americana, tomato, Heinz.

**The reveal:** The word is Hokkien **kôe-chiap / kôe-kap — fish brine from fermented fish/soy**, a SE Asian (Southern Chinese/Fish-sauce-adjacent) condiment label. British traders met it in Asia (1690s ⚠), tried to copy it without the fish — mushroom ketchup, walnut ketchup — England had *mushroom ketchup* as the normal condiment for a century. Only in **1800s America** did someone put the tomato immigrant in it, and Heinz (1876) sugar-loaded the New World version. Every language then got the *Chinese* name for a *British* fake of an *Asian* sauce, now filled with an *Aztec* fruit — with *Caribbean* sugar.

**Kicker:** One word carries four continents of food migration: Hokkien → London's mushroom phase → Philadelphia's tomato phase → the world's refrigerator door. It's the shortest etymology demo of the whole thesis.
