# S18: England's national drink was stolen from China, grown with Indian labor, paid for with opium — and arrived in a Portuguese princess's luggage

**The assumption:** tea = quintessential English since time out of mind (5 o'clock!).

**The reveal:** tea entered English court with **Catherine of Braganza's dowry, 1661** (Portuguese route, China's leaf — ⚠). To break China's monopoly: **Robert Fortune, 1848–51, smuggled plants, seeds and Chinese tea-makers out** to Darjeeling/Assam ⚠; Assam plantations ran on brutal indentured ("coolie") labor (⚠). And the trade imbalance was force-cleared with **opium grown in Bengal, sold into China → the Opium Wars**. "Afternoon tea" standardizes only 1840s (Anna Marie Russell claim ⚠). The cup = three empires stacked: sugar (S-mintz, plantation), tea (stolen), etiquette (invented).

**Proof stamps:** 1661 dowry → 1839/56–60 Opium Wars → 1848 Fortune theft → 1850s– Assam plantation boom → 1900s Tetley/Lipton branding.

**Kicker:** no other single daily habit carries so much: botanical theft, two wars, indentured labor, a queen's luggage — inside the most "polite" routine in the world. Pair with S19 (Vienna's coffee = Ottoman sacks) for a "cups of empire" double.
