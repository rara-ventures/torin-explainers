# S25: Sushi is 200-year-old Edo street fast food — and your "wasabi" is dyed American horseradish

**The assumption:** sushi = millennial Japanese refinement; wasabi = its essential companion.

**The reveal:** nigiri-zushi = **1820s–40s Edo stalls** (Hanaya Yohei tradition ⚠) — fast food from vinegar rice (preservation tech), rice that itself arrived from China's wet-paddy complex. The "ancient" global sushi boom is **1970s–80s** (LA's *California roll* hiding the raw fish because Americans wouldn't eat it ⚠ — Canada's Japanese-Canadian roll claims too). Wasabi: real *Eutrema* is slow, rare, mostly grated in high-end Japan; the green paste globally = **horseradish + starch + dye**, much of it grown in the **Pacific Northwest of the US** (Oregon "wasabi" fields supplying Japanese tables ⚠). 

**Proof stamps:** 700s *narezushi* Southeast-Asian ferment ✔ → 1820s Edo nigiri ⚠ → 1966 LA Kawafuku ⚠ → 1970s roll camouflage ⚠ → 2000s conveyor global → wasabi-horseradish supply chains.

**Kicker:** the most "pure Japanese" food on Earth was Edo fast food, re-engineered in Los Angeles, and served with a root vegetable that's basically American. The passport says Japan; the stamps say: where else.
