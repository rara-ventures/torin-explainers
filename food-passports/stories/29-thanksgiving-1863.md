# S29: Thanksgiving is an 1860s magazine invention — with a Mexican bird in the middle

**The assumption:** 1621 Pilgrims + turkey = America's most ancient tradition.

**The reveal:** the 1621 meal was one three-day harvest gathering; no turkey-legend followed for **200 years**. The holiday was *manufactured* by **Sarah Josepha Hale**, Godey's Lady's Book editor, in a decades-long letter campaign — Lincoln proclaimed it **1863** partly as Civil War nation-building ⚠. And the "traditional" menu is an exchange checklist: **turkey = Mexican domestication** (Aztec *totolin*; the name itself comes from the mislabeled guinea-fowl/"turkey-cock" trade route ⚠), squash & maize = Americas, cranberry sauce = only after **canning** (1860s ⚠), pumpkin pie = Wampanoag technique + colonist ovens + imported spice. Potatoes were *absent* from "the first Thanksgiving" — the very symbol of North American comfort food didn't exist there yet.

**Proof stamps:** 1621 single event ✔ → 1700s "turkey as national bird" Franklin aside ⚠ → 1846–1863 Hale campaign → 1863 Lincoln → 1900s can cranberry → 1941 TV-era standardized menu.

**Kicker:** America's least commercial "family" holiday is the most commercial: invented by a *magazine editor*, standardized by a *president*, sealed by *canning* — around a bird whose name is a trading-post error. Chain with S14/S28: war-and-media rations become everyone's heritage.
