# S33: The grain the exchange displaced is now the "superfood" — and Nepal is reversing it in real time

**The assumption:** kodo (finger millet) = poor-person's grain; rice & maize = development.

**The reveal:** maize and potato rewired the mid-hills in ~150 years (S06, S07 routes), pushing millet/buckwheat down the status ladder — "backward" food. Then the market loop closed: **UN International Year of Millets 2023** (India-led campaign ⚠ stats), climate-resilience science, and export demand turned kodo ko laddu, *fapar ko rotika*, and *jhangora* into **superfood-shelf items priced above the rice that replaced them**. Nepali startups & state agriculture now market millets as heritage nutrition — while maize keeps eating the hills. The Columbian Exchange is *being un-done as a product*, in your lifetime, at your own grocery counter.

**Proof stamps:** 1800s– maize/potato hill expansion → 1960s–70s HYV programs ⚠ (CIP/CIMMYT lines) → 1990s millet "neglect" stats ⚠ → 2023 IYM → 2020s– Nepali millet branding wave ⚠ (find 1–3 concrete brands).

**Kicker:** your great-grandmother ate kodo because maize hadn't conquered the pantry yet; you'll eat it because the exchange finally got expensive. Same field, opposite direction, one generation apart. This is the *only* story here happening live in Nepal — strongest local-content lane (anchor with S07 + S23 for "superfood is just an immigrant with good PR").
