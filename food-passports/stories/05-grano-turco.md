# S5: "Polenta" is a Mexican grass with a fake name

**The assumption:** Polenta = peasant Italy since Roman times.

**The reveal:** *Polenta* the word is ancient (farro mush); *polenta* the dish is maize — from Mexico — and Italians called it **grano turco, "Turkish grain"**, because it arrived via Ottoman/Iberian routes and nobody knew where it really started. It was bird and pig feed, then the Veneto poor's staple by the 1600s, then *the* rustic Italian icon. Its criminal record: pellagra epidemics when the poor ate maize-only diets ⚠1700s+.

**Proof stamps:** 1493→ Spain → mid-1500s Italy ⚠ → 1600s poenta of the poor → 1730s pellagra → 1900s heritage brand.

**Kicker:** When Tuscans insulted Venetians as "polentoni," they were mocking them for eating Mexico. The dish later apologized so thoroughly it became the symbol of Italy — and the insult became pride.

**Twist:** chestnut flour polenta (the *real* ancient mountain version) survived — Liguria's farro bread polenta made a comeback; the immigrant erased even its predecessor's name.
