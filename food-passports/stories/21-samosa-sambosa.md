# S21: The samosa's passport: Central Asian camel food, and its filling only arrived with the potato

**The assumption:** the aloo samosa = Indian street eternity (and Kathmandu's "samosa" feels ancient).

**The reveal:** the pastry ancestor is **Central Asian/Persian *sambosa*** — fried dough of the caravan routes, recorded by 14th-c. travelers and court cooks (Ibn Battuta serves *samusa* to a sultan; Amir Khusrau praises it for nobles ⚠ exact citations). Meat fillings came first. The **potato filling — the default today — requires the Andean tuber**, i.e. post-1700s–1800s adoption (S07's route), plus Amazonian chili for the "spicy" version (Door 1, S12). So the aloo samosa = a 700-year-old caravan pastry + a 500-year-old tuber + a 400-year-old chili, sold where all three routes met: Delhi, and every Kathmandu corner.

**Proof stamps:** 900s–1300s Turkic-Persian *sambosa* texts ⚠ → 14th c. Delhi sultanate courts ⚠ → 1800s potato-filling standardization ⚠ → 20c. Newa-Indian fusion in Nepal.

**Kicker:** the samosa is the Columbian Exchange folded into a triangle — and its *name* is older than Columbus while its *contents* are younger. Food like this is history you can bite.
