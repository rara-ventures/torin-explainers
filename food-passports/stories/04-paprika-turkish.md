# S4: The spice Hungary got from the Turks, who got it from Columbus

**The assumption:** Paprika is Hungary's ancient soul; goulash "always" had it.

**The reveal:** The chili entered 1500s Ottoman Hungary as **"Turkish pepper"** (*törökpaprika* — the name still confesses). Grown by Serbian gardeners in Bácska, eaten by the poor, distrusted by nobles who used black pepper. Then the 1800s nationalist era rebranded the Turk as a Hungarian: Szeged/Kalocsa mills, cookbook canon, and in the 20th century **EU protected names for "Hungarian paprika"** — over a pepper whose etymology blames Turkey and whose biology blames the Americas.

**Proof stamps:** 1493 "pepper" named → 1520s–60s Ottoman gardens ⚠ → 1600s peasant powder tech ⚠ → 1775 first printed recipes ⚠ → 1800s national canon → PDO era.

**Kicker:** Hungary sued Europe over "real" paprika. The word on its own passport is a lie written in three languages: *America → Turk → Magyar.*

**Nepal crossover:** same spice, opposite story — timur was *displaced* by it; Hungary *promoted* it to king.
