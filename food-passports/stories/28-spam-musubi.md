# S28: Spam — the ration that became "native" food of the Pacific

**The assumption:** Hawaiian musubi / Guam's spam culture = island tradition.

**The reveal:** Hormel's canned spiced ham = **1937** ⚡; its island identity was forged entirely by **WWII military supply chains** ( Guam as base; Okinawa's *spam musu*; Korea's *budae jjigae* — the "army stew" made from US-preservation surpluses near bases ⚠). Today **Guam ranks among the world's top per-capita Spam consumers** (⚠ verify) — a Hormel marketing legend — and the product's "native" status is so complete that island kitchens and Korean bases both count it as *their* tradition. The same WWII ration-to-heritage pipeline that made carbonara (S14) and Panda Express (S15).

**Proof stamps:** 1937 product → 1941–45 Pacific logistics → 1950s Okinawa/Korea base economies → 1980s musubi format (Hawaiian-Japanese deli invention ⚠) → present "local food."

**Kicker:** empires leave bases, bases leave canned goods, grandchildren call the cans heritage. Spam is the Columbian Exchange at *product* speed: 90 years from factory to folklore — the old crops needed 200. Same math as S33's millet reversal, but in a tin.
