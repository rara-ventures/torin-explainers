# S34: Chai is a double hijack — India stole back the tea the British stole from China

**The assumption:** masala chai = ancient Indian tradition (and "chai" proves India always drank tea).

**The reveal:** the leaf is Chinese in name and technique — but *assamica* is native to Assam, and **Maniram Dewan guided the British to it (1823–30s)** before Fortune's 1848 heist (S18) built the plantations. Indians themselves barely drank tea until the **Indian Tea Association invented the habit** (1901+ marketing, "tea break" propaganda, post-Swadeshi reframing of a foreign leaf as national product ⚠). Then the second hijack: the industry's prim British recipe (weak, little milk) was overwritten at the **tapri** — boiled milk, obscene sugar (S18/Mintz again), ginger-cardamom-clove from India's own ancient spice lanes. Industry archives derided the "Indian style" as vulgar through the 1910s–20s ⚠, then capitulated — because the people refused the original. Same inversion as S24 (MSG: Japan's science → China's industry) and S31 (state brands a food; the street brands it back).

**Proof stamps:** early 1800s tea = Company import, elite curiosity → 1823–35 assamica "discovery" w. Maniram Dewan ⚠ → 1848–51 Fortune theft (S18) → 1901– ITA domestic campaigns → 1916/1920s Ilam-Nepal gardens via Darjeeling planters ⚠ (Chyangma bari dating — research) → 1920s–30s industry accepts "Indian style" ⚠ → 1950s–60s "chai break" push → today tapri = national institution; UK's own chai-style (tetley+boil+milk) reversed back into Britain via diaspora (S17 sibling loop).

**Kicker:** China made the leaf, Britain made the plantation, and India made the *product* — over the loud objections of both. "Chai" is what happens when a colonized country colonizes the colonizer's crop on the way to the kettle. Nepal's chiya is that chain's final stop: assamica + sugar + tapri, never once touching China's teacup.
