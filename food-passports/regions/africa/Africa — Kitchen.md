# Kitchen: Africa (West · Central · East)

The continent that absorbed the most of the exchange — through the worst of it. Cassava, maize, peanut, chili all arrived on Portuguese ships tied to the slave trade, then African farmers made them *African* faster than any state could document: cassava = Congo Basin identity (*fufu*, *saka-saka* with cassava leaf — *verify*), groundnut = Yoruba/Senegambia protein system (*groundnut stew*, *kuli-kuli* — *verify*), maize = ugali plateau (W5-era) and *ogi*, scotch bonnet chili = the heat in jollof's tomato-pepper base. Tomato + pepper + onion — the *stew* trinity of West African cookery, standardized 19–20c. via trade paste (*verify*). Meanwhile banana/plantain is the *honest* ancient immigrant (pre-Columbian). **Africa proves the thesis hardest: its staples are the most thoroughly naturalized on Earth because adoption happened without written permission.**
