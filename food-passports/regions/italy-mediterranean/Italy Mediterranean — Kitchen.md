# Kitchen: Italy & Mediterranean

The world's most successful *passport swap*. Pre-1492 the peninsula had the honest half: olive, wheat pasta (durum via Arab Sicily — *verify*), cheese, wine, garum, chestnut, farro. The symbols added later: tomato (Mexico), polenta maize (Mexico), borlotti bean (Americas), zucchini (Mexico→Italy re-invented 1900s), even chili in 'nduja & arrabbiata (New World). **La cucina italiana as marketed = ~half Columbian Exchange.**

## Pre-immigrant pantry
- Wheat (Fertile Crescent, ancient), barley, olive, vine, chickpea/lentil/fava (Old World), cheese, cured pork, garum
- Heat: black pepper (Indian), ginger (via spice route), long pepper — no capsicum
- *Polenta* before maize: farro, then chestnut flour in mountain Liguria

## Adoption summary
| Crop | Door | When | Citizenship proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tomato | Spain→Naples (viceregal) | 1544 ornamental; 1692 recipe; 1830s pizza | San Marzano DOP; "Made in Italy" paste brand |
| Maize | via Ottoman/Iberian routes | 1500s feed → 1600s poenta | Veneto identity dish, name still says *grano turco* |
| Common bean | via Spain | 1500s–1600s | pasta e fagioli; *fagiolo* named "India" — wrong passport, honest route |
| Squash/zucchini | Americas→Italy→world | zucchini cultivar 1900s Italy — a *reverse* immigrant | the "Italian" vegetable Mexico never saw |

## Script hook
"Italy didn't receive the tomato — it received the copyright. Four centuries of stamps, then the brand."
