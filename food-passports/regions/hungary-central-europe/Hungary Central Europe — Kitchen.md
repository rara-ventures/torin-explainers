# Kitchen: Hungary & Central Europe

**Paprika is the world's best case study in rebranding.** The chili arrived with the Ottomans as *törökpaprika* ("Turkish pepper") — grown in Bácska Serbian gardens, used by the poor, distrusted by nobles — then the 18th–19th c. nationalist era *renamed the Turk a Hungarian*: Szeged/Kalocsa powdered industry, Gundel cookbooks, gulyás/paprikás as national canon, and 20c. EU protected names. Potato arrived Habsburg-promoted (Maria Theresa era — *verify*), New World beans married cabbage and lard in peasant one-pots (*babs* — *verify dishes*), tomato joined the base. Hungary's "eternal" spice rack is ~350 years old and blames Turkey in its own language.
