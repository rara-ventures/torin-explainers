# Kitchen: India & the Goan Estado

**Goa is Door 1 of the whole exchange.** The Portuguese Estado da Índia (Goa capital 1510) carried New World crops into every harbor it touched — and Indian cooking absorbed them with a speed that still stuns: chili so thoroughly vernacular by the 1600s that Ayurvedic/Siddha texts had to classify a *new* hot spice; the "Mughlai" repertoire later adopted tomato-onion gravy bases (post-1800s cookbooks — *verify*), potato curry & *aloo paratha* are 19–20c. (*verify*). **India today is the world's largest chili producer & consumer — a country eating the Amazon.**

## Pre-immigrant pantry
- Black pepper (Malabar — the reason anyone came), long pepper/pippali, ginger, mustard; Sichuan-relative tibet pepper in NE
- Rice, millets, Old World legumes (mung, urad, pigeon pea — *truly* Indian ✔), sugarcane (India's gift to the world)
- "Vegetables": gourds, eggplant (Indian origin ✔), okra; no capsicum, no potato, no tomato, no peanut
