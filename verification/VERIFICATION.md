## 2. Molniya Orbit 63.4° — *Frozen Orbit* ✅

- **Torin:** `why-does-russia-use-satellites-in-strange-orbits-to-watch-for-nuclear-launches.md:34-38` — 63.4°, apogee 40,000 km, 718 min, Van Allen loops.
- **Verified:** `dω/dt ∝ (4 - 5 sin²i)` → zero at `i = arcsin(√(4/5)) = 63.435°` (also 116.57° retrograde). Apogee 39,750 km (kidder et al. 1992), perigee ~600 km, eccentricity 0.737, period 717.7 min. Frozen orbit requires no perigee drift; 3 satellites give 24h cover; earliest Molniya 1964, 160+ launched, ~50 remain as debris hazard (Kolyuka et al. ISSFD 2009).
- **Added nuance:** Torin's "Earth's bulge drags apogee" is J2 first-order term — correct simplification.
